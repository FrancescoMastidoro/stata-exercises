## Background
The `nlsy80` dataset contains cross-sectional data from the 1980 National Longitudinal Survey of Youth, covering wages, education, cognitive scores, family background, and demographic characteristics for 935 individuals. Assessing data quality — locating missing values, scrutinising variables with partial non-response, and checking that numeric values fall within plausible ranges — is an essential early step before any labour economics analysis, because silent data problems can distort every estimate that follows.

## Your tasks

1. **Audit missingness across the dataset** — Load the dataset with `webuse nlsy80, clear` and run `misstable summarize`. Identify which variables contain missing values and record the exact missing counts as comments in your do-file. Which variable has the largest number of missing observations, and what share of the total 935 observations does that represent?

2. **Examine the distribution of a key continuous variable** — Run `summarize wage, detail` on the variable `wage` (monthly earnings). Record the minimum, maximum, mean, and median. Is the distribution symmetric or skewed? Write a one-sentence comment explaining what the relationship between the mean and median implies about the shape of the earnings distribution.

3. **Flag missing background variables and examine their correlates** — Generate a new indicator variable called `missing_feduc` that equals 1 if `feduc` (father's education) is missing and 0 if it is non-missing. Use `tabulate missing_feduc` to confirm your count matches the reference figure from Task 1. Then use `summarize educ` separately for the two groups — do respondents with missing father's education tend to have higher or lower own education than those for whom father's education is observed?

4. **Detect potentially implausible values in hours and IQ** — Run `summarize hours, detail` and `summarize iq, detail`. For `hours`, count how many observations report more than 60 hours per week using `count` with an appropriate `if` condition. For `iq`, verify whether any observations fall outside the range 50–145 (the documented range). Write comments in your do-file interpreting whether the extreme values you find warrant further investigation in a labour economics context.

5. *(Bonus)* **Cross-tabulate missingness patterns** — Generate a second indicator `missing_meduc` that equals 1 if `meduc` (mother's education) is missing and 0 otherwise. Then use `tabulate missing_feduc missing_meduc` to examine whether missingness in father's education and missingness in mother's education tend to co-occur. Compute the mean `lwage` separately for the four cells of this two-way table using `summarize lwage if ...` conditions. Write a comment discussing whether the missingness pattern appears random or whether it may be systematically related to labour market outcomes.
