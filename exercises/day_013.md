## Background
The `mroz87` dataset is a classic sample from the 1987 Panel Study of Income Dynamics, containing information on married women's labor supply, wages, education, and family characteristics. Visualization is an essential step in labor economics research — before running any regression, plotting your variables helps you spot distributional quirks, outliers, and patterns that summary statistics alone can obscure.

## Your tasks

1. **Histogram of wife's hourly wage** — Load the dataset with `webuse mroz87, clear`. Draw a histogram of `wwage76` (wife's hourly wage in 1976). Add a title to the graph and label the x-axis clearly. Note in a comment whether the distribution looks approximately symmetric or skewed.

2. **Scatter plot of wages and education** — Create a scatter plot with `wedyrs` (wife's years of education) on the x-axis and `wwage76` on the y-axis. Add meaningful axis labels and a title. Do you notice any pattern — does wage tend to increase with education?

3. **Add a linear fit to the scatter** — Rebuild the scatter plot from Task 2, but this time overlay a linear fit line using `twoway`. Use the `lfit` plot type alongside `scatter` within a single `twoway` command. This gives you a visual sense of the education–wage gradient.

4. **Bar chart of labor force participation by education group** — First, generate a binary education variable called `high_educ` that equals 1 if `wedyrs` is greater than or equal to 12 (completed high school or more) and 0 otherwise. Then use `graph bar` to display the mean of `lfp` (wife's labor force participation) separately for `high_educ == 0` and `high_educ == 1`. Add a title and interpret the result in a comment.

5. *(Bonus)* **Overlaid wage distributions by labor force participation** — Using `twoway`, overlay two kernel density plots of `wwage76`: one for women with `lfp == 1` and one for `lfp == 0`. Use different line patterns or colors to distinguish the two groups, add a legend, and write a comment interpreting what you observe. Think carefully about what `wwage76` captures for women who did not work in 1976 before drawing conclusions.
