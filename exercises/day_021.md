## Background
The Mroz (1987) dataset is a classic in labor economics, drawn from the PSID and containing detailed information on married women's labor force participation, wages, and household characteristics. Mastering `if`/`in` conditions and `bysort` is essential for any researcher who needs to compute group-level statistics, restrict samples, or compare subpopulations — tasks that arise constantly in applied work.

## Your tasks

1. **Inspect participation and wages** — Load the dataset and use `tabulate` to count how many women are in the labor force (`lfp == 1`) versus not. Then use `summarize wwage76` with an `if` condition to display summary statistics *only for women who participated in the labor force* (`lfp == 1`). Note what happens to the sample size.

2. **Compare education groups** — Use `bysort` together with `summarize` to compute the mean and standard deviation of `wwage76` separately for each level of wife's education in years (`wedyrs`). Then restrict the same command using `if lfp == 1` so that you only summarize wages for working women.

3. **Create and explore a kids indicator** — Generate a binary variable called `has_young_kids` equal to 1 if the woman has at least one child under age 6 (`kl6 > 0`), and 0 otherwise. Use `tabulate has_young_kids lfp` to produce a cross-tabulation and inspect whether having young children is associated with lower labor force participation.

4. **Group means with a combined condition** — Using `bysort smsa`, compute the mean of `faminc` (family income) separately for women inside and outside an SMSA. Then add a combined `if` condition to restrict this to women who are in the labor force **and** have at least one child under 6 (`kl6 > 0`), so you are comparing family income for working mothers in and out of urban areas.

5. *(Bonus)* **Within-group ranking** — For working women only (`lfp == 1`), use `bysort wedyrs` and `egen` with the `rank()` function to create a variable `wage_rank` that ranks each woman's 1976 wage (`wwage76`) within her education group. Then use `list wedyrs wwage76 wage_rank in 1/20` to inspect the first 20 rows of the result.

## Hints

<summary>Click to reveal hints (try first!)</summary>

- **Hint 1 (Tasks 1–2)**: The `if` qualifier in Stata comes *after* the varlist and *before* any options — for example, `summarize varname if condition`. When combining `bysort` with `if`, the `if` qualifier still goes after the command and varlist, not inside the `bysort` parentheses.
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: You can generate a binary variable with `generate has_young_kids = (kl6 > 0)`. Because `kl6` has no missing values in this dataset, this expression is safe — every observation will be assigned either 0 or 1 with no ambiguity.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: To combine two conditions with AND, use `&` between them inside the `if` clause: `if condition_a & condition_b`. Remember that `bysort varname:` precedes the command, and the `if` qualifier still follows the command name and varlist.
- **Hint 4 (Bonus)**: `egen wage_rank = rank(wwage76) if lfp == 1` together with `bysort wedyrs:` will rank within education groups. Think carefully about where the `if` goes relative to `bysort` — you may need to restrict the dataset before sorting, or use the `if` qualifier directly on the `egen` line.
