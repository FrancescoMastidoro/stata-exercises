# Day 23: Conditional Logic and Group Operations

**Dataset**: `sysuse cancer`
**Estimated time**: ~25 minutes

## Background
This dataset records survival outcomes from a drug trial of 48 cancer patients, including the drug assigned, patient age, and whether the patient died during the study period. Mastering `if`/`in` conditions and `bysort` is essential in labor economics and applied research whenever you need to compare subgroups, restrict samples, or compute group-level statistics efficiently.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the groups** — Load the dataset and tabulate the `drug` variable. Then separately tabulate `died` for each drug group using `if` conditions (e.g., `tab died if drug == 1`). How does the share of deaths vary across the three drug groups?

2. **Restrict with `in`** — Display (using `list`) the `studytime`, `drug`, and `age` variables for only the first 10 observations using the `in` range qualifier. Then repeat but show only observations 39 through 48. What do you notice about the ordering?

3. **Group summaries with `bysort`** — Use `bysort drug:` to compute the mean of `studytime` and `age` for each drug group using `summarize`. Then store the mean study time per drug group in a new variable called `mean_studytime_drug` using `bysort drug: egen`.

4. **Combine `bysort` and `if`** — Among patients who **died** (`died == 1`), use `bysort drug:` to count the number of such patients per drug group (hint: think about `egen` with a counting function, applied to the restricted group). Compare this with the counts you found in Task 1.

5. *(Bonus)* **Flag above-average survivors** — Create a variable `long_survivor` equal to 1 if a patient's `studytime` is strictly greater than the overall mean `studytime`, and 0 otherwise. Then use `bysort drug:` to compute the share of long survivors within each drug group, and display the result with `tabstat`.
