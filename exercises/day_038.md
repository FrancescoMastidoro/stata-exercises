# Day 38: Two-Sample t-Tests, OLS Regression, and Marginal Effects

**Dataset**: `sysuse bpwide`
**Estimated time**: ~25 minutes
**Difficulty**: ⬛⬛⬛⬛⬜

## Background
The `bpwide` dataset contains fictional blood-pressure readings for 120 patients measured before and after a treatment, along with demographic information such as sex and age group. Comparing group means, fitting regressions, and computing marginal effects are core inferential tools in labor economics and program evaluation — practicing them on a clean dataset with no missing values lets you focus purely on the syntax and interpretation.

## Your tasks

1. **Summarize by group** — Use `tabstat` to report the mean, standard deviation, and minimum/maximum of `bp_before` and `bp_after` separately for each level of `sex`. Do the two groups look different before treatment?

2. **Two-sample t-test** — Run an independent-samples t-test comparing `bp_before` between male and female patients (use the `by(sex)` syntax of `ttest`). Report the t-statistic, the p-value for the two-sided test, and state in one sentence whether you reject equality of means at the 5 % level.

3. **OLS regression** — Regress `bp_after` on `bp_before`, `sex`, and the three age-group dummies implied by `agegrp` (use `i.agegrp` to let Stata handle the reference category automatically). Interpret the coefficient on `bp_before` and the coefficient on `sex`.

4. **Marginal effects with `margins`** — After the regression from Task 3, use `margins agegrp` to obtain the average predicted value of `bp_after` for each age group, holding all other variables at their observed values. Then use `marginsplot` to visualize the result. What does the pattern across age groups suggest?

5. *(Bonus)* **Interaction term** — Re-estimate the regression from Task 3, but add an interaction between `sex` and `i.agegrp`. Use `margins sex#agegrp` to compute predicted blood pressure for every sex–age-group cell, and `marginsplot` to display the interaction. Does the effect of age group differ by sex?

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: After `regress`, type `margins agegrp` with no additional options; this computes adjusted predictions at the means of all other covariates. Follow it immediately with `marginsplot` — Stata remembers the last `margins` result automatically.
