## Background
The NLSW 1988 dataset contains wage and employment information for a sample of women in the United States, making it a natural sandbox for labor economics inference. Understanding how to move from raw regression coefficients to interpretable marginal effects is a core skill in any applied econometrics workflow.

## Your tasks

1. **Descriptive t-test** — Test whether mean wages (`wage`) differ significantly between college graduates (`collgrad == 1`) and non-graduates (`collgrad == 0`). Report the t-statistic and state your conclusion at the 5% level.

2. **Baseline OLS regression** — Regress `wage` on `collgrad`, `ttl_exp`, `tenure`, and `south`. Interpret the coefficient on `collgrad` in plain English. Note: `tenure` has 15 missing values — check how many observations are actually used in estimation.

3. **Add a control and test joint significance** — Extend the regression from Task 2 by adding `married` and `union`. After estimating, use `test` to jointly test whether the coefficients on `married` and `union` are both zero. What do you conclude?

4. **Marginal effects with an interaction** — Re-run the regression from Task 3 but add an interaction term between `collgrad` and `south` (i.e., `c.collgrad#c.south`). Then use `margins` with the `dydx` option to compute the average marginal effect of `collgrad` on wages across all observations. Compare this number to the simple coefficient on `collgrad` from Task 2 — why might they differ?

5. *(Bonus)* **Predicted wages by group** — Using the model from Task 4, use `margins collgrad south` (without `dydx`) to obtain predicted mean wages for each combination of `collgrad` and `south`. Then use `marginsplot` to visualize the results. Describe what the plot reveals about the college premium across regions.

- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: When an interaction is present, the coefficient on `collgrad` alone is no longer the average marginal effect — `margins, dydx(collgrad)` correctly averages the effect over the joint distribution of `collgrad` and `south` in the data.
