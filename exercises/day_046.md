# Day 46: Fixed Effects and Within-Group Estimation

**Dataset**: `webuse grunfeld`
**Estimated time**: ~25 minutes

## Background
The Grunfeld dataset is a classic panel dataset recording investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large US firms over 20 years (1935–1954). Panel methods like fixed effects are essential in labor and industrial economics because they let you control for unobserved, time-invariant heterogeneity across units — a core skill for credible causal inference.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset and use `xtset` to declare `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Then run `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Inspect within-group variation** — Use `xtsum` on `invest`, `mvalue`, and `kstock`. For each variable, note the *between* standard deviation and the *within* standard deviation. Which variable has the largest within-company variation relative to its overall mean?

3. **Estimate a pooled OLS and a fixed-effects model** — Regress `invest` on `mvalue` and `kstock`. First run a standard `regress` (pooled OLS), then run `xtreg` with the fixed-effects option (`fe`). Compare the coefficients on `mvalue` and `kstock` across the two specifications. Do the estimates change substantially?

4. **Test whether fixed effects are needed** — After the `xtreg, fe` estimation, use the built-in F-test that Stata reports at the bottom of the output to assess whether the firm fixed effects are jointly significant. Then also run `xtreg` with the random-effects option (`re`) and perform a Hausman test to compare the FE and RE estimators. Based on the Hausman test result, which specification is preferred?

5. *(Bonus)* **Absorb fixed effects with `areg`** — Re-estimate the fixed-effects model using `areg` instead of `xtreg, fe`, absorbing `company`. Verify that the coefficients on `mvalue` and `kstock` are identical to those from `xtreg, fe`. Then check whether the reported R² and degrees of freedom differ between the two commands, and briefly comment in a Stata note (`// comment`) on why they might differ.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: The syntax for declaring a panel is `xtset panelvar timevar`. After declaring, `xtdescribe` shows you gaps and balance — look for the phrase "strongly balanced" in the output.
- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: `xtsum varlist` decomposes variance into between (across companies) and within (over time, within the same company) components. A within SD that is large relative to the overall mean suggests lots of time-series movement inside each firm.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: To run a Hausman test, store the FE estimates first with `estimates store`, then run `xtreg, re`, and finally call `hausman` with the stored FE name as the first argument. A significant chi-squared statistic favors the FE model.
- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: `areg depvar indepvars, absorb(groupvar)` is the syntax. The key difference in reported R² relates to how each command accounts for the absorbed group means when computing residuals.
