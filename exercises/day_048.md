# Day 48: Fixed Effects and Panel Estimation

**Dataset**: `webuse regsmpl`

## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of women from the National Longitudinal Survey tracked from 1968 to 1988, containing wage and employment information across multiple years. Panel methods like fixed effects regression are fundamental in labor economics because they allow you to control for unobserved, time-invariant individual characteristics — such as ability or motivation — that would otherwise bias your estimates.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare it as a panel with `idcode` as the individual identifier and `year` as the time identifier. After declaring, run `xtdescribe` and note what it tells you about the panel's balance (is every individual observed in every year?).

2. **Pooled OLS baseline** — Run a simple pooled OLS regression of `ln_wage` on `ttl_exp`, `tenure`, `grade`, and `black`. Store the results in memory using `estimates store pooled`. Note that `tenure` and `grade` have missing values — observe how Stata handles the reduction in sample size automatically.

3. **Random effects and fixed effects models** — Using the same dependent variable and regressors as Task 2, estimate both a random effects model (`xtreg, re`) and a fixed effects model (`xtreg, fe`). Store them as `re_model` and `fe_model` respectively. Compare the coefficients on `ttl_exp` and `tenure` across the three stored models using `estimates table pooled re_model fe_model`.

4. **Hausman test** — Run the Hausman test to formally compare the random effects and fixed effects estimates. The test asks whether the random effects estimator is consistent. Based on the test statistic and p-value, which model does the test favor, and why does that conclusion matter for interpreting the returns to experience and tenure in this sample?

5. *(Bonus)* **Within-individual variation with `areg`** — Re-estimate the fixed effects model using `areg` with `absorb(idcode)` instead of `xtreg, fe`. Confirm that the coefficient estimates on `ttl_exp` and `tenure` match those from your `xtreg, fe` model. Then think about — and write as a comment in your do-file — one reason a researcher might prefer `areg` over `xtreg, fe` in practice, and one limitation of `areg` compared to `xtreg`.
