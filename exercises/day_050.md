## Background
The Grunfeld dataset is a classic panel used in econometrics, tracking investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. firms over 20 years (1935–1954). Panel methods like fixed effects are essential in labor and firm-level economics because they remove time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity that would otherwise bias OLS estimates.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset and use `xtset` to declare `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Then use `xtdescribe` to verify the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Run a pooled OLS regression** — Regress `invest` on `mvalue` and `kstock` using plain `regress`. Note the R² and the coefficients. This is your baseline — it ignores firm-level heterogeneity entirely.

3. **Estimate a fixed effects model** — Re-run the same regression using `xtreg, fe`. Compare the coefficients on `mvalue` and `kstock` to your pooled OLS results from Task 2. Do they change substantially? Report which estimate you would trust more and why, in a comment.

4. **Replicate fixed effects with `areg`** — Use `areg` with `absorb(company)` to run the same regression. Confirm that the coefficients on `mvalue` and `kstock` are numerically identical to those from `xtreg, fe`. Then explain in a comment what the practical difference between `areg` and `xtreg, fe` is in terms of what each command reports in its output header.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test for the relevance of fixed effects** — After your `xtreg, fe` estimation, run the built-in F-test for the null hypothesis that all firm fixed effects are jointly zero (Stata reports this automatically at the bottom of `xtreg, fe` output). Then also run `xtreg` with the `re` option (random effects) and use `hausman` to test whether fixed or random effects is the preferred specification. Interpret the result in a comment.
