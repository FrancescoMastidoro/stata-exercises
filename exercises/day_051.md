# Day 51: Difference-in-Differences (DiD)

**Dataset**: `webuse psidextract`
**Estimated time**: ~25 minutes
**Difficulty**: ⬛⬛⬛⬛⬛

## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 panel observations on workers across 7 time periods, including wages, union status, and demographic characteristics. Difference-in-differences is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, used to estimate causal effects by comparing changes over time between treated and untreated groups.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset and use `xtset` to declare the panel with `id` as the unit identifier and `t` as the time variable. Then use `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is balanced. How many individuals and how many periods are there?

2. **Create a post-period indicator** — Generate a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `t >= 5` and 0 otherwise. Then tabulate `post` against `union` to get a sense of how union status distributes across early and late periods.

3. **Construct the DiD estimator manually** — Using `bysort`, compute the mean of `lwage` for each combination of `union` (0/1) and `post` (0/1). Store your results mentally or in a matrix. Then manually calculate the DiD estimate as: (mean wage for union workers post − mean wage for union workers pre) − (mean wage for non-union workers post − mean wage for non-union workers pre). Report the four cell means and the DiD figure.

4. **Estimate DiD via regression** — Run an OLS regression of `lwage` on `union`, `post`, and their interaction `union#post` (using factor variable notation). Confirm that the coefficient on the interaction term matches your manual DiD estimate from Task 3. Then add the controls `ed`, `exp`, `occ`, `south`, and `smsa` to the regression and report how the interaction coefficient changes.

5. *(Bonus)* **Fixed-effects DiD** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg, fe` (absorbing individual fixed effects) instead of pooled OLS. Include `post`, the `union × post` interaction, and the same controls as Task 4. Compare the interaction coefficient to the pooled OLS result. Why might the two estimates differ, and which is more credible for causal inference in this context?

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse psidextract`, the command `xtset id t` declares the panel. `xtdescribe` will display the panel pattern — look at the line showing the fraction of observations present in all periods.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: Use `tabstat lwage, by(post) stats(mean)` separately for union and non-union subsamples, or combine groups with `egen group = group(union post)` and then `tabstat lwage, by(group)`. The DiD formula is a simple double difference of the four resulting means.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: Factor variable notation in Stata uses `##` for main effects *and* interaction together: `regress lwage i.union##i.post`. The interaction coefficient is the one labeled `1.union#1.post`.

- **Hint 4 (Bonus)**: After `xtset id t`, use `xtreg lwage i.post i.union#i.post ed exp occ south smsa, fe`. Note that time-invariant regressors (like `blk` or `fem`) would be dropped by the within-estimator — think about why individual fixed effects absorb unobserved heterogeneity across workers.
