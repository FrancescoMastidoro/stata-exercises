## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed across survey years from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, experience, and geographic indicators. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is one of the most widely used quasi-experimental strategies in labor economics, exploiting variation across groups and over time to identify causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure using `idcode` as the individual identifier and `year` as the time variable. After declaring it, run `xtdescribe` to check how balanced the panel is.

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `year` is greater than or equal to 80 (i.e., 1980 onwards), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `post` against `year` to confirm it was created correctly.

3. **Define a treatment group and the interaction term** — Use the variable `black` as a proxy for a "treated" group (as if a policy differentially affected Black women after 1980). Create a new variable called `did` as the product of `black` and `post`. This is the classic DiD interaction term. Summarize `black`, `post`, and `did`.

4. **Run the DiD regression** — Regress `ln_wage` on `black`, `post`, and `did`, clustering standard errors by `idcode`. Interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it represent in the DiD framework? Also add `ttl_exp` as a control variable and compare how the estimate on `did` changes.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add individual fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg` with the `fe` option, including `post`, `did`, and `ttl_exp` as regressors (note: `black` drops out under individual FE since it does not vary within person). Compare the `did` coefficient to the pooled OLS result from Task 4 and discuss why the estimates might differ.

## Hints

<summary>Click to reveal hints (try first!)</summary>

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After loading the data, the syntax for declaring a panel is `xtset panelvar timevar`. Once set, `xtdescribe` gives you a visual summary of which individuals appear in which periods — look at the "%" column to assess balance.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: The DiD interaction term is simply the product of the treatment dummy and the post dummy. You can create it with `gen did = black * post`. This variable will equal 1 only for treated individuals in the post period.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: To cluster standard errors by individual when using `regress`, add `vce(cluster idcode)` as an option. Remember: the coefficient on `did` captures the *differential change* in log wages for Black women after 1980, relative to non-Black women — this is the "treatment effect" in the DiD logic.

- **Hint 4 (Bonus)**: With `xtreg, fe`, Stata absorbs all time-invariant individual characteristics. Since `black` never changes within a person, it is collinear with the fixed effects and is automatically dropped. Focus your comparison on the `did` coefficient across specifications.
