# Day 53: Instrumental Variables (IV) with `ivregress`

## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains information on female labor supply, including female and male education, number of children, non-labor income, and female earnings. Instrumental variables estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor economics for addressing endogeneity — a situation where a regressor is correlated with the error term, invalidating OLS inference.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Note the ranges of `fem_inc`, `fem_educ`, `other_inc`, `kids`, and `male_educ`. Then run a simple OLS regression of `fem_inc` on `kids` and `other_inc` and record the coefficient on `kids`.

2. **First-stage intuition** — Before running IV, check whether `male_educ` is a plausible instrument for `kids` by regressing `kids` on `male_educ` and `other_inc`. Report the coefficient on `male_educ`, its t-statistic, and comment on whether it appears to be a relevant instrument (i.e., whether it is correlated with the endogenous variable).

3. **2SLS estimation** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to estimate the effect of `kids` on `fem_inc`, treating `kids` as endogenous, using `male_educ` as the excluded instrument and `other_inc` as an exogenous control. Compare the 2SLS coefficient on `kids` to the OLS coefficient you found in Task 1 — does the direction or magnitude change?

4. **Diagnostic tests** — After your `ivregress` command, run `estat firststage` to retrieve the first-stage F-statistic and assess instrument strength using the conventional threshold of F > 10. Then run `estat endogenous` to test whether `kids` is actually endogenous (interpret the p-value at the 5% level). Summarise what both tests tell you together.

5. *(Bonus)* **LIML comparison** — Re-estimate the same model using `ivregress liml` instead of 2SLS. Compare the LIML and 2SLS estimates on `kids`. Look up why LIML can be preferred when instruments are weak and briefly comment on whether that concern applies here given your first-stage F-statistic.

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: The `ivregress 2sls` syntax separates exogenous controls from the instrument inside parentheses — the endogenous variable goes on the left of the `=` sign inside the parentheses, and the instrument goes on the right. The form looks like `ivregress 2sls depvar controls (endogvar = instrument)`.
- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: `estat firststage` and `estat endogenous` must be run *immediately* after `ivregress` before any other estimation command overwrites `e()`. The endogeneity test null hypothesis is that the variable *is* exogenous — a small p-value means you reject exogeneity.
