## Background
The Mroz 1987 PSID dataset contains detailed information on married women's labor supply, earnings, and household characteristics — a classic benchmark for IV estimation in labor economics. Instrumental variables are essential when a regressor (like education) is endogenous due to omitted ability bias or reverse causality, and valid instruments allow consistent identification of causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the key variables** — Load the dataset and summarize `lwage`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, `smsa`, and the potential instruments `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs`. Report the mean and standard deviation of each. Note that `lwage` is observed for all 753 observations here (no missing values), which differs from many wage datasets.

2. **Run the OLS baseline** — Regress `lwage` on `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `smsa`. Store the coefficient on `wedyrs` and note the standard error. This is your benchmark before addressing endogeneity.

3. **Run the IV regression** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to instrument `wedyrs` with `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs`, keeping `wexper`, `kl6`, and `smsa` as exogenous controls. Compare the IV coefficient on `wedyrs` to the OLS estimate — does the direction of bias match what you would expect from ability bias?

4. **Test instrument relevance** — After the `ivregress` command, run `estat firststage` to inspect the first-stage F-statistic. Based on the conventional threshold of F > 10, assess whether your instruments appear strong or weak.

5. *(Bonus)* **Sargan–Hansen overidentification test** — Since you have two instruments for one endogenous variable, the model is overidentified. Run `estat overid` after the IV regression and interpret the p-value: what does failing to reject the null imply about instrument validity?
