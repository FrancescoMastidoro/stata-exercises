## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations tracking 595 workers across 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects when treatment is not randomly assigned, exploiting variation across groups and over time.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `id` as the unit identifier and `t` as the time variable. Confirm the panel is balanced by running `xtdescribe`.

2. **Create a post-period indicator** — The panel covers 7 time periods (`t` runs from 1 to 7). Create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `t` is greater than or equal to 4, and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `post` against `t` to verify your coding is correct.

3. **Define a treatment group and construct the DiD interaction** — Use `union` as the treatment indicator (workers who are ever unionized will serve as the "treated" group). Create a variable called `union_ever` equal to 1 for individuals who have `union == 1` in *any* period of the panel, using `egen` with the `max()` function by `id`. Then construct the DiD interaction term `did = union_ever * post`.

4. **Estimate the DiD regression** — Run an OLS regression of `lwage` on `union_ever`, `post`, and `did`. Then re-estimate the same model adding controls for `ed`, `exp`, `fem`, and `blk`. Compare the coefficient on `did` across the two specifications and interpret its sign and magnitude in economic terms.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add individual fixed effects** — Re-estimate the controlled DiD model using `xtreg lwage post did ed exp, fe`. Compare the coefficient on `did` to your pooled OLS estimates from Task 4. Explain in a comment why the fixed-effects estimator absorbs `union_ever` and why that is actually desirable in this context.

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: To identify workers who are *ever* unionized, you need to collapse variation within each individual to a single summary value — `egen union_ever = max(union), by(id)` is the right pattern because `max()` will return 1 if the worker was ever in a union across their 7 observations.

- **Hint 2 (Task 5)**: In a fixed-effects model, any variable that does not vary *within* an individual over time is collinear with the individual fixed effects and gets dropped automatically. Think about whether `union_ever` varies within a person by construction, and what that implies for identification of the DiD coefficient.
