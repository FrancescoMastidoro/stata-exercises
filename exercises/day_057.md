## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations on 595 workers observed over up to 7 time periods, with variables covering wages, union membership, industry, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is one of the most widely used quasi-experimental strategies in labor economics, allowing researchers to estimate causal effects by comparing changes over time across treated and untreated groups.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset`, and use `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel structure. How many individuals are observed in all 7 periods?

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `post` equal to 1 if `t >= 5` (the "post" period) and 0 otherwise. Then tabulate `post` against `union` to get a sense of how union membership is distributed across periods.

3. **Compute group-period means** — Use `tabstat lwage`, broken down by `union` and `post` (use the `by()` option or run separate `tabstat` calls), to manually compute the four group-period cell means needed for a back-of-the-envelope DiD estimate. Calculate the DiD by hand: (mean_union_post − mean_union_pre) − (mean_nonunion_post − mean_nonunion_pre).

4. **Estimate the DiD regression** — Run a regression of `lwage` on `union`, `post`, and their interaction `union#post` (using factor notation), controlling for `ed`, `exp`, `blk`, and `fem`. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term. Then re-run the same model adding individual fixed effects with `xtreg, fe` — does the interaction coefficient change meaningfully?

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends check** — Restrict the sample to `t <= 4` (the pre-period only). Generate a variable `t_union = t * union` and regress `lwage` on `union`, `t`, `t_union`, `ed`, `exp`, `blk`, and `fem`. A coefficient on `t_union` close to zero and statistically insignificant would be consistent with the parallel trends assumption. What do you find?

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: In Stata factor notation, an interaction between two binary variables is written `i.union##i.post`. For the fixed-effects version, switch to `xtreg lwage i.union##i.post ed exp blk fem, fe` — remember that time-invariant variables like `blk` and `fem` will be absorbed by the fixed effects.
