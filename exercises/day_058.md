## Background
The NLSY80 dataset is a 1980 cross-section of 935 workers containing wages, education, experience, and demographic characteristics drawn from the National Longitudinal Survey of Youth. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, used to estimate the causal effect of a treatment by comparing changes across groups and over time — or, in a cross-sectional analogue, by leveraging variation in group membership and an interaction term.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the treatment group** — Load the dataset and tabulate the `college` variable. Then compute the mean of `lwage` separately for college and non-college workers. What is the raw wage gap between the two groups?

2. **Construct a DiD interaction term** — In a DiD framework, the treatment effect is captured by an interaction. Create a new variable called `college_south` that is the product of `college` and `south`. Verify its distribution with `tab college_south`.

3. **Run the DiD regression** — Regress `lwage` on `college`, `south`, and `college_south`. Interpret each coefficient: what do the main effects represent, and what does the coefficient on `college_south` capture in terms of the DiD estimator?

4. **Add control variables and compare** — Extend the regression from Task 3 by adding `exper`, `tenure`, `married`, and `urban` as controls. Does the DiD estimate (`college_south`) change materially? Use `estimates store` to save both specifications and then display them side by side with `esttab` or `estimates table`.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption heuristically** — Within the `south == 1` subsample, run a regression of `lwage` on `college`, `exper`, and `black`. Then do the same within `south == 0`. Compare the college premium across regions — if DiD is valid, pre-treatment trends should not differ systematically. What does the difference in college coefficients across subsamples suggest about the credibility of your DiD design?
