## Background
The Grunfeld dataset tracks investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. firms across 20 years (1935–1954), making it a classic panel used to study firm investment behavior. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone of modern causal inference in labor and corporate economics, allowing you to estimate treatment effects by comparing changes over time across treated and control groups.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset`, and summarize all variables. Identify how many firms there are, how many years are covered, and confirm the panel is balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a "post" dummy equal to 1 for years after 1944 (i.e., `year >= 1945`) and a "treated" dummy equal to 1 for companies with `company <= 5` (the five larger firms by convention). Generate the DiD interaction term `did = treated * post`.

3. **Estimate a basic DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and the `did` interaction term using `regress`. Interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it represent in terms of the average treatment effect on the treated (ATT)?

4. **Absorb firm and year fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg, fe` to control for firm fixed effects, and then add year dummies (using `i.year`) in a pooled OLS regression with `regress`. Compare the DiD coefficient across these two specifications and note how controlling for unobserved firm heterogeneity changes your estimate.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (treated vs. control) and `year`. Then use `twoway line` to plot average investment over time for each group. Comment on whether the pre-1945 trends look roughly parallel, which is required for DiD identification to be credible.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse grunfeld`, use `xtset company year` to declare the panel structure. `xtdescribe` or `xtsum` can tell you whether the panel is balanced.
- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: You can generate the dummies with `gen post = (year >= 1945)` and `gen treated = (company <= 5)`. The interaction is simply the product of the two dummies — remember that in Stata you can also write `gen did = treated * post` directly.
- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: In a two-period DiD, the `did` coefficient captures the average change in `invest` for the treated group relative to the control group, net of common time trends. Think about which comparison the four "cells" (treated/control × pre/post) represent.
- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: For the fixed-effects model, use `xtreg invest post did, fe` (note: `treated` drops out in FE because it is time-invariant within firm). For the OLS version with year dummies, use `regress invest treated post did i.year` and compare the `did` coefficient between specifications.
