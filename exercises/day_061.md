## Background
The PSID extract tracks 595 individuals across 7 periods, recording wages, union status, job characteristics, and demographics — making it ideal for panel methods. Difference-in-differences is a cornerstone of modern labor economics, used to evaluate how treatment (such as union coverage or industry switching) causally affects outcomes by comparing within-person change across groups over time.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset with `webuse psidextract`. Use `xtset` to declare the panel with `id` as the individual identifier and `t` as the time variable. Then use `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is strongly balanced (all 595 individuals observed at all 7 periods).

2. **Define a "post" period** — Create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `t >= 5` and 0 otherwise. Then use `tabulate t post` to verify that your indicator correctly splits the panel into a pre-period (`t` 1–4) and a post-period (`t` 5–7).

3. **Identify a treatment group** — Use `union` as your treatment variable (workers covered by a union contract). Create a variable `treated` equal to 1 for individuals who are in a union in *any* wave, using `egen` with the `max()` function over `id`. Tabulate `treated` to see how many individuals are ever-union versus never-union.

4. **Estimate the DiD regression** — Regress `lwage` on `treated`, `post`, and the interaction `treated#post` (or equivalently, construct `did = treated * post` manually and include all three terms). Add individual fixed effects by including `i.id` or using `xtreg, fe`. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term as the DiD estimate of union coverage on log wages.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Collapse the data to cell means of `lwage` by `treated` and `t`. Plot the two wage trajectories (one for `treated == 1`, one for `treated == 0`) over time using `twoway line`. Do the pre-period trends (`t` 1–4) look parallel? Briefly comment in your do-file using `/* */` comments.

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: `egen` has a `max(varname)` function that, when used with `by(groupvar)`, computes the within-group maximum — a 1 anywhere in the group means the individual was ever treated. Look up `help egen` and the `by()` option syntax.
- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: With panel data you can absorb individual fixed effects cleanly with `xtreg lwage ... , fe` after `xtset`. This wipes out all time-invariant differences between workers, so only `post` and the interaction remain the key identifying regressors — `treated` alone will be dropped by the FE estimator.
