## Background
The Mroz (1987) dataset is a classic labor economics dataset drawn from the PSID, containing information on married women's labor force participation, wages, hours worked, education, and family characteristics. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics when OLS is biased due to endogeneity — here, a wife's wage is likely correlated with unobserved ability, making husband's and wife's family background variables natural instruments.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the endogeneity problem** — Load the dataset and run a simple OLS regression of `whrs75` (wife's hours worked in 1975) on `wwage75` (wife's hourly wage in 1975), `kl6`, `k618`, `wifeage`, and `wedyrs`. Report the coefficient on `wwage75` and explain in a comment why this estimate may be biased.

2. **Check instrument relevance** — A valid instrument must be correlated with the endogenous variable. Regress `wwage75` on the proposed instruments `hedyrs` (husband's education) and `hmedyrs` (wife's mother's education), together with the control variables `kl6`, `k618`, `wifeage`, and `wedyrs`. Report the F-statistic and comment on instrument strength using the conventional threshold of F > 10.

3. **Run the IV regression** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to estimate the effect of `wwage75` on `whrs75`, instrumenting `wwage75` with `hedyrs` and `hmedyrs`, and including `kl6`, `k618`, `wifeage`, and `wedyrs` as exogenous controls. Compare the IV coefficient on `wwage75` to your OLS estimate from Task 1.

4. **Test for endogeneity and overidentification** — After the `ivregress` command, run the Durbin–Wu–Hausman endogeneity test using `estat endogenous` and the Sargan–Hansen overidentification test using `estat overid`. Interpret both results in comments: does the endogeneity test suggest OLS is inconsistent? Does the overidentification test cast doubt on instrument validity?

5. *(Bonus)* **Use LIML as a robustness check** — Re-estimate the IV model from Task 3 using Limited Information Maximum Likelihood (`ivregress liml`) instead of 2SLS. Compare the LIML and 2SLS coefficients on `wwage75`. Look up why LIML is preferred when instruments are weak, and note whether the two estimates differ meaningfully given your Task 2 findings.

- **Hint 1 (Task 2)**: The first-stage F-statistic is not automatically reported by a simple `regress`; after running `regress wwage75 hedyrs hmedyrs kl6 k618 wifeage wedyrs`, use `test hedyrs hmedyrs` to obtain the joint F-statistic for the excluded instruments specifically.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: The `ivregress 2sls` syntax separates endogenous regressors from instruments inside parentheses — the structure is `ivregress 2sls depvar controls (endogenous = instruments)`. Make sure the exogenous controls appear *outside* the parentheses as well.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: Both `estat endogenous` and `estat overid` must be run immediately after `ivregress` before any other estimation command overwrites the results. The Sargan test only provides meaningful information when you have more instruments than endogenous variables (i.e., the model is overidentified), which is the case here with two instruments and one endogenous regressor.
