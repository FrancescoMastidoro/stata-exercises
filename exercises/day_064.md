## Background
The Grunfeld dataset tracks investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 U.S. firms over 20 years (1935–1954), making it a classic panel used to study firm-level investment behavior. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor and industrial economics for estimating causal treatment effects when a clean experiment is unavailable.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare it as a panel with `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm Stata recognizes the panel correctly by running `xtsum invest`.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1–5 (the "treatment group") and 0 for companies 6–10. Then create a post-period indicator `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 otherwise. Tabulate both variables to confirm they are balanced across companies and time periods.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run the baseline DiD regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Run a basic OLS regression of `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Interpret the coefficient on `did` as your DiD estimate.

4. **Add fixed effects to the DiD** — Re-run the DiD regression using `xtreg` with the `fe` option to absorb company fixed effects, and include `post` and `did` as regressors (note: `treated` drops out with `fe`). Compare the coefficient on `did` to the estimate from Task 3 and briefly note whether controlling for firm-level heterogeneity changes the result meaningfully.

5. *(Bonus)* **Event-study style check** — Instead of a single `post` dummy, create a variable `rel_year = year - 1945` representing years relative to the "treatment" onset. Run `xtreg invest c.rel_year##i.treated, fe` and plot the marginal effects using `margins, dydx(treated) at(rel_year=(-10(1)9))` followed by `marginsplot`. Discuss what a linear pre-trend (or lack thereof) would imply for the parallel trends assumption.
