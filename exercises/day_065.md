## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations on 595 workers tracked over 7 periods, with information on wages, union status, industry, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects when treatment is not randomly assigned, by exploiting variation in treatment timing across units.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare the panel** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then declare the panel structure using `xtset` with `id` as the unit identifier and `t` as the time variable. Confirm Stata recognizes it as a balanced panel by inspecting the output.

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `post` equal to 1 for periods `t >= 5` and 0 otherwise. Then tabulate `union` by `post` to describe how union membership varies across the two periods. Report the share of union members in pre- and post-periods.

3. **Construct the DiD estimate manually** — Compute the four group-period means of `lwage` (union × post cells) using `tabulate` or `table`. Then calculate by hand the DiD estimate: (mean wage for union workers post − mean wage for union workers pre) − (mean wage for non-union workers post − mean wage for non-union workers pre). Report your numeric result.

4. **Estimate DiD via regression** — Run an OLS regression of `lwage` on `union`, `post`, and their interaction `union#post` (use factor-variable notation). Add controls for `ed`, `exp`, `fem`, and `blk`. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term and compare it to your manual DiD estimate from Task 3.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add individual fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg, fe` to absorb worker-level fixed effects (replacing the `union` main effect, which is now collinear with the fixed effects). Compare the interaction coefficient to the pooled OLS result from Task 4 and briefly explain why the estimates may differ.

## Hints
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal hints (try first!)</summary>

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: The command to declare a panel is `xtset panelvar timevar`. After running it, Stata will tell you whether the panel is strongly balanced — look for that word in the output.
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: You can use `table post union, stat(mean lwage)` (Stata 17+) or `tabulate post union` combined with `bysort post union: summarize lwage` to retrieve the four cell means you need for the manual calculation.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: Factor-variable notation for an interaction in Stata is `i.union##i.post`. This automatically includes both main effects and the interaction term. The coefficient on `1.union#1.post` is your DiD estimate.
- **Hint 4 (Bonus)**: With `xtreg, fe`, Stata absorbs all time-invariant characteristics of each worker. Because `union` varies over time for some workers, it is *not* dropped — but the `union` main effect loses its cross-sectional interpretation. Focus your comparison on the interaction term.
