## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, experience, and demographic variables. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is one of the most widely used identification strategies in labor economics, allowing you to estimate causal effects by comparing changes over time across groups.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare panel structure** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset`, and run `xtdescribe` to understand the panel's coverage. How many individuals and time periods are in the data?

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a "post-1978" indicator variable called `post` that equals 1 for years strictly after 1978 and 0 otherwise. Then create a "high education" treatment group indicator called `highschool` that equals 1 if `grade` is greater than or equal to 12 (note that `grade` has 2 missing values — handle them appropriately with `if !missing(grade)`). Tabulate `highschool` to confirm the split.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term and run the baseline regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did = highschool * post`. Regress `ln_wage` on `highschool`, `post`, and `did`. Report and interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it represent in the DiD framework?

4. **Add individual and time fixed effects** — Re-estimate the regression using `xtreg ln_wage post did, fe` (within-estimator), which absorbs individual fixed effects. Compare the coefficient on `did` to the pooled OLS result from Task 3. Then add year dummies by replacing the single `post` variable with `i.year` and re-run `xtreg`. Does the DiD estimate change substantially?

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Compute the mean of `ln_wage` by `year` and `highschool` using `collapse` (save the data first with `preserve`/`restore`). Plot the two group-level wage trends over time using `twoway line`. Do the pre-1978 trends look parallel? Discuss informally what you observe.

- **Hint 2 (Tasks 3–4)**: In a standard DiD setup, `highschool` captures the time-invariant group difference, `post` captures the common time trend, and `did` captures the differential change — the causal estimate of interest. When you switch to `xtreg, fe`, the fixed effects absorb `highschool` (it is time-invariant within individuals), so you will only see `post` and `did` in the output — this is expected and correct.
