## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains firm-level investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. corporations observed annually from 1935 to 1954. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor and applied economics, allowing researchers to estimate causal effects by comparing treated and control units before and after an intervention.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset`, and describe the time and firm dimensions. Report the range of `year` and how many firms are in `company`.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Create a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company` > 5 (the "treated" group) and 0 otherwise. Then create a `post` dummy equal to 1 for years after 1944 (i.e., `year >= 1945`) and 0 otherwise. Tabulate both new variables to verify they look as expected.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term** — Generate an interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Then run a simple OLS regression of `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Report the coefficient on `did` and explain in a comment what it represents.

4. **Add firm and year fixed effects** — Re-estimate the model from Task 3 using `xtreg` with firm fixed effects (`fe`) and manually including year dummies (use `i.year`). Compare the coefficient on `did` to the one from Task 3. In a comment, explain why fixed effects matter for this setting.

5. *(Bonus)* **Visualize parallel trends** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (`treated`) and `year`. Then use `twoway line` to plot mean investment over time for treated and control firms on the same graph, adding a vertical reference line at `year == 1944`. Comment on whether the parallel trends assumption looks plausible.
