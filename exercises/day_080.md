## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, including women's income, education, partner's education, children, and employment status. Instrumental variables estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor economics when OLS is suspected to be biased due to endogeneity — for example, when education or income is correlated with unobserved ability or preferences.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Then estimate a simple OLS regression of `fem_inc` on `fem_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc`. Note the coefficient on `fem_educ` and its standard error for later comparison.

2. **First-stage regression** — Run a regression of `fem_educ` on `male_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc`. Report the coefficient and t-statistic on `male_educ`. Explain in a comment why `male_educ` might be a valid instrument for `fem_educ` (relevance condition).

3. **IV estimation with `ivregress 2sls`** — Estimate the effect of `fem_educ` on `fem_inc` using `male_educ` as an instrument for `fem_educ`, controlling for `kids` and `other_inc`. Write the correct `ivregress 2sls` syntax with the parenthetical notation for the endogenous variable and its instrument.

4. **Test instrument strength** — After the `ivregress` command, use `estat firststage` to retrieve the first-stage F-statistic. Check whether it exceeds the conventional threshold of 10. Then compare the IV coefficient on `fem_educ` to the OLS coefficient from Task 1 — add a comment describing whether endogeneity appears to be present and in which direction.

5. *(Bonus)* **Overidentification test** — Add `fem_work` as a second instrument alongside `male_educ` (re-run `ivregress 2sls` with both instruments). Then run `estat overid` to perform the Sargan–Hansen test. Report the p-value and write a comment explaining what it means for the joint validity of your instruments.
