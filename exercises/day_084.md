## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed across survey years from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, experience, and demographic controls. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing across groups to recover causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare the panel** — Load the dataset with `webuse regsmpl`, then declare the panel structure using `xtset`. Confirm the time variable runs from 1968 to 1988 and check how many unique individuals are in the panel using `xtsum` on `idcode`.

2. **Create a post-period indicator** — Define a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 for survey years 1978 and later (i.e., `year >= 78`), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `post` against `year` to verify the cutoff is correct.

3. **Define a treatment group based on education** — Create a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 if an individual has more than 12 years of education (`grade > 12`), and 0 otherwise. Because `grade` has 2 missing values, use `replace treated = .` to handle missing cases explicitly. Tabulate `treated` and note the group sizes.

4. **Estimate the DiD model** — Run an OLS regression of `ln_wage` on `treated`, `post`, and their interaction (`treated#post`), including `ttl_exp` and `black` as controls. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term as the DiD estimate. Then re-estimate the same model adding individual fixed effects using `xtreg, fe` and compare the two interaction coefficients — do they differ substantially?

5. *(Bonus)* **Event-study style check** — Instead of a single `post` dummy, create year dummies for each survey year and interact them with `treated`. Run an OLS regression of `ln_wage` on these interaction terms (omitting one pre-period year as baseline), plus `ttl_exp` and `black`. Plot the interaction coefficients across years using `coefplot` (if installed) or `marginsplot` after `margins`. Discuss what a flat pre-trend in the pre-period coefficients would imply for the parallel trends assumption.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse regsmpl`, use `xtset idcode year` to declare the panel. Then try `xtsum idcode` — the "n" reported there tells you the number of unique individuals.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: Remember that `grade` has 2 missing values. After your `gen treated = (grade > 12)`, Stata will code observations where `grade` is missing as `treated = 0` by default (since missing is treated as a very large number in numeric comparisons). Use `replace treated = . if missing(grade)` to propagate the missingness correctly.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: In Stata, you can write the interaction between two binary variables as `c.treated#c.post` (if both are continuous) or use factor notation `i.treated##i.post`. For the fixed-effects model, `xtreg ln_wage i.treated##i.post ttl_exp black, fe` will absorb individual-level time-invariant variation — notice what happens to the `treated` main effect and why.
