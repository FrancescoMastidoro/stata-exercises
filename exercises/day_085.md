# Day 85: Instrumental Variables with Labor Supply Data

## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains information on 500 women's labor supply decisions, including their income, their partners' education, and the number of children they have. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of causal inference in labor economics, used whenever an explanatory variable is endogenous — correlated with the error term due to omitted variables, reverse causality, or measurement error.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Identify which variables are binary outcomes, which are continuous, and what the plausible range of `other_inc` is. Note that `other_inc` ranges from negative values to over 100.

2. **OLS baseline** — Regress `fem_inc` on `kids`, `other_inc`, and `fem_educ`. Report and briefly interpret the coefficient on `kids` in a comment. This is your naive OLS estimate, potentially biased if fertility is endogenous to female income.

3. **First-stage regression** — Run a regression of `kids` on `male_educ`, `other_inc`, and `fem_educ`. Test whether `male_educ` is a relevant instrument by checking its t-statistic and reporting the F-statistic of the first stage. A strong instrument typically has an F-statistic above 10.

4. **IV estimation with `ivregress`** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to estimate the effect of `kids` on `fem_inc`, instrumenting for `kids` with `male_educ` and controlling for `other_inc` and `fem_educ`. After the regression, run `estat firststage` to retrieve the first-stage F-statistic and compare it to the rule-of-thumb threshold. Comment on how the IV coefficient on `kids` differs from your OLS estimate in Task 2.

5. *(Bonus)* **Hausman-style endogeneity test** — After your `ivregress` estimation, use `estat endogenous` to formally test whether `kids` is endogenous. Interpret the p-value: if it is below 0.05, OLS is inconsistent and IV is preferred. Then re-estimate using `ivregress liml` instead of `2sls` and compare the LIML coefficient on `kids` to the 2SLS result — do they differ meaningfully?
