## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains firm-level investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large US corporations observed annually from 1935 to 1954 — a classic balanced panel used in applied econometrics. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor and industrial economics, exploiting variation across units and time to recover causal treatment effects under the parallel trends assumption.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel** — Load the dataset with `webuse grunfeld`. Declare the panel structure using `xtset`, then summarize `invest`, `mvalue`, and `kstock`. Report the number of firms, the number of time periods, and confirm the panel is balanced.

2. **Construct a treatment indicator** — Create a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company` ≤ 5 (the "treated" group) and 0 otherwise. Then create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 for years after 1944 (`year` > 1944) and 0 otherwise. These two variables will form the basis of your DiD design.

3. **Compute the raw DiD estimate by hand** — Using `tabstat` or `collapse` (do not run a regression yet), compute the mean of `invest` for all four cells: treated/control × pre/post. Then manually calculate the DiD estimate as (treated_post − treated_pre) − (control_post − control_pre). Report the four group means and the resulting DiD estimate in a comment.

4. **Run the DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and their interaction `treated#post` (use factor-variable notation). Also include `mvalue` and `kstock` as controls. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term and its standard error. Then re-run the same regression with firm fixed effects (`i.company`) instead of the `treated` dummy — explain in a comment why the `treated` dummy drops out in that specification.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test parallel trends visually** — Restrict the sample to the pre-treatment period (`year` ≤ 1944). Using `collapse`, compute mean `invest` by `company` group (treated vs. control) and `year`. Then use `twoway line` to plot the average investment trends for both groups over the pre-period and inspect whether the parallel trends assumption looks plausible. Save the graph as `figures/day_088_pretrends.png`.

## Hints

- **Hint 1** (Task 3): `tabstat invest, by(post)` only gives you one dimension — you need to condition on both `treated` and `post` simultaneously. Consider using `tabstat` with `if` conditions for each of the four cells, or `table treated post, stat(mean) c(mean invest)` to see all four means at once before doing the arithmetic by hand in a comment.

- **Hint 2** (Task 4): In Stata, you write an interaction between two binary variables as `treated##post` (using `##`) to automatically include both main effects and the interaction, rather than manually typing three separate terms. When you add `i.company` as firm fixed effects, Stata absorbs all time-invariant firm characteristics — and since `treated` is a fixed firm-level dummy that never changes across time, it is perfectly collinear with the firm fixed effects and will be dropped automatically.
