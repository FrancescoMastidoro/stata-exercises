## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed repeatedly from 1968 to 1988, containing log wages, education, experience, and geographic indicators. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects when treatment assignment is non-random but follows a clear timing pattern.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load `webuse regsmpl`, then declare the panel structure using `xtset`. Confirm the panel identifier and time variable, and summarize `ln_wage`, `black`, `south`, and `ttl_exp` to get a sense of the data.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — In many DiD applications, a policy "turns on" at a specific time. Create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `year >= 78` (i.e., on or after 1978) and 0 otherwise. Then tabulate `post` against `year` to verify it was created correctly.

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Use `black` as the group indicator (treated = Black women) and `post` as the time indicator. Regress `ln_wage` on `black`, `post`, and their interaction `c.black#c.post`. The coefficient on the interaction term is your DiD estimate. Interpret its sign and magnitude.

4. **Add controls and use `xtreg`** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg` with individual fixed effects (`fe`), controlling for `ttl_exp` and `south` (which has 8 missing values — Stata will drop those observations automatically). Compare the interaction coefficient from this model to the one in Task 3 and explain why it changes.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `ln_wage` by `black` and `year`. Then use `twoway line` to plot mean log wages over time separately for Black and non-Black women on the same graph. Does the pre-1978 trend look parallel? What would a violation imply for your DiD estimate?

## Hints
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal hints (try first!)</summary>

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: In Stata, `c.black#c.post` creates the interaction of two continuous (or 0/1) variables. You can also write `gen interaction = black * post` manually and include it — both approaches yield the same coefficient.
- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: `xtreg ln_wage ... , fe` absorbs all time-invariant individual characteristics. Notice that `black` itself is time-invariant, so it will be dropped by the FE estimator — this is expected and does not indicate an error.
- **Hint 3 (Task 5)**: Use `preserve` before `collapse` so you can restore the full dataset afterward. The syntax `collapse (mean) ln_wage, by(black year)` computes group-year means, and `twoway (line ln_wage year if black==0) (line ln_wage year if black==1)` draws both series.
