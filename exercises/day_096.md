## Background
The Mroz (1987) PSID dataset is a classic benchmark in labor economics, containing detailed information on 753 married women's labor supply, wages, education, and family background. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is essential when a regressor — such as education — is endogenous due to omitted ability or family background effects, and valid instruments must affect the endogenous variable but have no direct effect on the outcome.

## Your tasks

1. **OLS baseline** — Load the dataset and estimate a simple OLS regression of `lwage` on `wedyrs` (wife's years of education) and `wexper` (work experience). Report the coefficient on `wedyrs` and note its magnitude.

2. **Check instrument relevance** — Wife's mother's education (`wmedyrs`) and wife's father's education (`wfedyrs`) are candidate instruments for `wedyrs`. Regress `wedyrs` on `wmedyrs`, `wfedyrs`, and `wexper`. Check whether the instruments are jointly significant using an F-test, and comment on whether the relevance condition appears satisfied.

3. **IV estimation via `ivregress`** — Estimate the same wage equation as in Task 1, but instrument `wedyrs` with `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs` using `ivregress 2sls`. Compare the IV coefficient on `wedyrs` to the OLS estimate — is it larger or smaller, and what might explain the direction of the difference?

4. **Test for endogeneity** — Use the Durbin–Wu–Hausman test (available via `estat endogenous` after `ivregress`) to formally test whether `wedyrs` is endogenous. State the null hypothesis, report the p-value, and interpret the result at the 5% significance level.

5. *(Bonus)* **Overidentification test** — Since you have two instruments for one endogenous variable, the model is overidentified. Run `estat overid` after your `ivregress` command to perform the Sargan–Hansen test. State what the null hypothesis of this test is, report the p-value, and discuss what it implies for the validity of your instruments.

## Hints
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal hints (try first!)</summary>

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: The syntax for `ivregress 2sls` follows the pattern `ivregress 2sls depvar exogvars (endogvar = instruments)` — the endogenous regressor goes inside parentheses together with its instruments after an equals sign.
- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: After running a regression, `test wmedyrs wfedyrs` performs a joint F-test on the two instruments simultaneously — a rule of thumb is that an F-statistic above 10 suggests instruments are not weak.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: `estat endogenous` must be typed immediately after `ivregress`; the null hypothesis is that the suspected endogenous variable is in fact exogenous, so a small p-value leads you to reject exogeneity.
- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: The Sargan–Hansen overidentification test has a null hypothesis that *all* instruments are valid (i.e., uncorrelated with the structural error); failing to reject is the desirable outcome here.
