## Background
The Mroz 1987 PSID dataset is a classic benchmark in labor economics, containing detailed information on married women's labor force participation, wages, and family structure. Instrumental variables estimation is essential when a regressor (such as education) is endogenous — correlated with unobserved ability or motivation — and a valid instrument allows us to isolate exogenous variation.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the key variables** — Load the dataset and summarize `wwage76`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `smsa`. Report the mean and standard deviation of each. Then tabulate `lfp` to understand what share of women in the sample are in the labor force.

2. **OLS benchmark** — Regress `wwage76` on `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `smsa` using only observations where `lfp == 1` (i.e., women who are working and have an observed wage). Save the OLS coefficient on `wedyrs` to compare later.

3. **First-stage regression** — Now treat `wedyrs` as potentially endogenous. Use `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs` (wife's mother's and father's years of education) as instruments. Run the first-stage OLS regression of `wedyrs` on `wmedyrs`, `wfedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `smsa` (restricting to `lfp == 1`). Report the F-statistic on the excluded instruments and comment on instrument relevance — is the rule-of-thumb threshold met?

4. **IV estimation and comparison** — Estimate the IV (2SLS) regression of `wwage76` on `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `smsa`, instrumenting `wedyrs` with `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs`, still restricting to `lfp == 1`. Use the `ivregress 2sls` command. Compare the IV coefficient on `wedyrs` with the OLS coefficient from Task 2 — in which direction does the bias go, and what does that suggest about the relationship between education and unobserved ability?

5. *(Bonus)* **Overidentification and weak instrument checks** — After the `ivregress` command, run `estat endogenous` to test whether `wedyrs` is actually endogenous (Hausman-style test), and `estat overid` to test the overidentifying restrictions (Sargan–Hansen test). Interpret both p-values: does the evidence support treating education as endogenous, and do the instruments appear valid?
