## Background
The Grunfeld dataset tracks investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. firms across 20 years (1935–1954), making it a classic panel used in econometrics textbooks. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is one of the most powerful quasi-experimental tools in labor and applied economics, allowing you to estimate causal effects by comparing treated and control groups before and after a policy change.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `company` as the unit identifier and `year` as the time variable. Summarize all variables to get a sense of their ranges.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Suppose a fictional policy affected companies 1–5 starting in 1945 (i.e., `year >= 1945`). Generate a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1–5, a binary variable `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and an interaction term `did` equal to `treated * post`. This is the classic DiD setup.

3. **Run the baseline DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Report and interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it represent in the DiD framework?

4. **Add controls and fixed effects** — Re-run the DiD regression including `mvalue` and `kstock` as controls, and add company fixed effects using `areg` with `absorb(company)`. Compare the coefficient on `did` to the baseline estimate from Task 3 and note whether it changes meaningfully.

5. *(Bonus)* **Event-study plot** — Instead of a single `post` dummy, generate a set of year-relative-to-treatment dummies (e.g., `year - 1945` ranging from −10 to +9) and interact each with `treated`. Regress `invest` on these interaction terms plus company fixed effects (absorbing `company`), omitting the period just before treatment (year 1944, i.e., relative year −1) as the baseline. Use `coefplot` (if installed) or manually `list` the coefficients to check whether pre-trends appear flat — a key assumption of DiD.

- **Hint 1 (Tasks 1–2)**: After `xtset company year`, use `generate treated = (company <= 5)` and `generate post = (year >= 1945)` — Stata evaluates logical expressions as 0/1 automatically. Then multiply them for the interaction.

- **Hint 2 (Tasks 3–4)**: In `areg`, the syntax is `areg invest treated post did mvalue kstock, absorb(company) robust`. Note that `treated` will be collinear with company fixed effects (it does not vary within company), so Stata will drop it — this is expected and correct. The `did` coefficient is your DiD estimate.
