## Background
The NLSY 1980 cross-section contains 935 observations on young workers, including monthly wages, education, IQ scores, family background variables, and labor market outcomes. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of labor economics because OLS estimates of the return to schooling are biased upward if more able individuals both earn more *and* acquire more education — IV allows us to isolate exogenous variation in education to recover a causal wage return.

## Your tasks

1. **Inspect the potential instruments** — Load the dataset, then use `summarize` and `tabulate` to explore `educ`, `meduc`, `feduc`, and `brthord`. Note how many observations are missing for each of these three potential instruments, and think about whether the sample size will shrink meaningfully when you use them.

2. **Estimate a naive OLS baseline** — Regress `lwage` on `educ`, `exper`, `tenure`, `black`, `south`, and `urban`. Report and interpret the OLS coefficient on `educ`. Remember that `lwage` is already in log form, so the coefficient has a percentage interpretation.

3. **Run a first-stage regression** — Regress `educ` on `meduc` (mother's education) as the instrument, plus the same controls (`exper`, `tenure`, `black`, `south`, `urban`). Check whether `meduc` is a strong predictor of `educ` by examining its t-statistic and by computing the first-stage F-statistic using `test meduc` immediately after the regression. A commonly used rule of thumb is F > 10.

4. **Estimate the IV model and compare** — Use `ivregress 2sls lwage (educ = meduc) exper tenure black south urban` to obtain the IV estimate of the return to schooling. Compare the IV coefficient on `educ` with the OLS coefficient from Task 2 and interpret the direction of any difference. Then run `estat firststage` to retrieve the first-stage diagnostics automatically.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test instrument validity with overidentification** — Re-estimate the IV model using *both* `meduc` and `feduc` as instruments for `educ` (keeping `exper`, `tenure`, `black`, `south`, `urban` as controls). After estimation, run `estat overid` to perform the Sargan–Hansen overidentification test. State the null hypothesis of the test, report the p-value, and explain what a failure to reject implies for instrument validity. Be mindful that `feduc` has 194 missing values and discuss how this affects your usable sample.
