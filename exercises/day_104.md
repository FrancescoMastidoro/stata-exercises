## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply decisions, including female income, male partner income, education levels, number of children, and employment status. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor economics, used to recover causal estimates when a regressor is endogenous — that is, when it is correlated with unobserved determinants of the outcome.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset with `webuse laborsup`. Use `describe` and `summarize` to understand all six variables. Note the range of `fem_educ` (8–16), `other_inc` (which includes negative values), and the binary outcome `fem_work`. Which variables might be jointly determined — i.e., potentially endogenous?

2. **Run a naïve OLS regression** — Regress `fem_inc` on `fem_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc` using `regress`. Interpret the coefficient on `fem_educ`: what does it suggest about the return to an additional year of female education? Store these results using `estimates store ols`.

3. **Instrument female education with male education** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to regress `fem_inc` on `kids` and `other_inc`, instrumenting `fem_educ` with `male_educ`. Store results as `estimates store iv`. How does the IV coefficient on `fem_educ` compare to the OLS estimate in magnitude and sign?

4. **Test instrument relevance and run the Hausman test** — After your `ivregress` command, run `estat firststage` to inspect the first-stage F-statistic for `male_educ`. Then use `hausman iv ols` to test whether the OLS estimate of `fem_educ` is statistically consistent. What do the results suggest about endogeneity?

5. *(Bonus)* **Compare IV estimators** — Re-estimate the IV model using the LIML estimator (`ivregress liml`) with the same specification. Use `estimates table ols iv liml, b se` to display all three sets of coefficients side by side. Do the 2SLS and LIML estimates differ notably here, and what does that tell you about the degree of weak-instrument concern?

Think carefully about which variable goes inside the parentheses as the endogenous regressor versus which is the instrument.

A conventional rule of thumb for a strong instrument is a first-stage F-statistic above 10. For `hausman`, the *consistent* estimator (IV) must be listed first, followed by the *efficient-under-H₀* estimator (OLS).
