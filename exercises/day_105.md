## Background
The Grunfeld dataset records annual investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large US firms over 20 years (1935–1954), making it a classic panel used in econometrics textbooks. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor and industrial economics, and practicing it on a balanced panel helps you understand how the estimator separates time trends from treatment effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare panel structure** — Load the dataset with `webuse grunfeld`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm the panel is strongly balanced using `xtdescribe`.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1–5 (the "treatment group") and 0 for companies 6–10 (the "control group"). Then create a binary `post` variable equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 otherwise. Verify the distribution of both variables with `tabulate`.

3. **Compute the raw 2×2 DiD estimate by hand** — Using `summarize` with `if` conditions, calculate the four group-period means of `invest`: treated pre, treated post, control pre, control post. Then manually compute the DiD estimate as (treated_post − treated_pre) − (control_post − control_pre) and record the value in a comment.

4. **Estimate DiD with a regression** — Run a regression of `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and their interaction `treated#post` (using factor-variable notation). Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term and compare it to your hand-calculated estimate from Task 3. Then re-run the regression adding company fixed effects (`i.company`) and year fixed effects (`i.year`) — note how the estimates change.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends visual check** — Using `collapse` (with `mean`), collapse the data to company-year means of `invest` by `treated` group and `year`. Then use `twoway line` to plot average investment over time separately for treated and control firms, and comment on whether the parallel trends assumption looks plausible in the pre-period (before 1945).

- **Hint 1 (Task 2)**: `generate treated = (company <= 5)` uses a logical expression that evaluates to 1 or 0 — the same approach works for `post` using a year threshold. Always `tabulate` new dummies to catch off-by-one errors.
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: Use `summarize invest if treated==1 & post==0` (and the other three combinations) to collect the four means. Write them down — this manual check is a great way to verify your regression output in Task 4.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: Factor-variable notation for an interaction in Stata is `i.treated##i.post`, which automatically includes both main effects and the interaction. To add two-way fixed effects, simply append `i.company i.year` to the regressor list.
