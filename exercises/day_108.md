## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed across survey years 1968–1988, containing log wages, education, experience, and geographic variables. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing or group membership across time to recover causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset with `webuse regsmpl`. Declare it as a panel using `xtset` with `idcode` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Then use `xtdescribe` to inspect the balance of the panel and report how many individuals appear in how many periods.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary "post" variable equal to 1 for years strictly after 1978 (i.e., `year > 78`) and 0 otherwise. Then define a "treated" group as individuals who are `black == 1`. Finally, create the DiD interaction term `did = treated * post`. Tabulate `post` and `did` to confirm your coding.

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Regress `ln_wage` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using `xtreg` with random effects (`re`). Interpret the coefficient on `did`: does it suggest that the log wage gap between Black and non-Black women narrowed or widened after 1978?

4. **Add controls and compare specifications** — Re-run the DiD regression adding `ttl_exp`, `grade`, and `south` as controls (note: `grade`, `south`, and `tenure` have some missing values — use `regress` instead of `xtreg` so you can clearly see the effective sample size). Compare the `did` coefficient with and without controls and comment on how omitting experience and education might bias the estimate.

5. *(Bonus)* **Event-study style check** — Instead of a single `post` dummy, create year dummies for `year == 73`, `year == 78`, `year == 83`, and `year == 88` (omitting one as the reference). Interact each with `treated` and regress `ln_wage` on these interaction terms plus `treated` and the year dummies, using `regress`. Plot (or tabulate) the interaction coefficients across years to informally assess whether a parallel pre-trends assumption is plausible.

- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: Use `gen post = (year > 78)` — Stata evaluates the Boolean expression as 0 or 1 automatically. Make sure `did` is the element-wise product of two binary variables, not a logical expression.

- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: In `xtreg ln_wage treated post did, re`, the `did` coefficient is your DiD estimator. A negative coefficient would mean the treated group's wage *fell* relative to the control group after the cutoff; a positive one means convergence.
