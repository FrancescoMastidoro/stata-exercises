## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains annual investment, market value, and capital stock data for 10 large U.S. firms from 1935 to 1954, making it a classic panel used to study firm investment behavior. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor and industrial economics for estimating causal effects when treatment is assigned at the group level over time.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset and declare it as a panel with `xtset`, using `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Then use `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Generate a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company <= 5` (the "treated" group) and 0 otherwise. Then generate a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 for years before 1945. Tabulate both variables to verify their distributions.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term and run the baseline regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using `regress`. Report what the coefficient on `did` represents in plain language.

4. **Improve the estimator with fixed effects** — Re-estimate the model using `xtreg` with firm fixed effects (`fe`). Include `post` and `did` as regressors (note: `treated` drops out with firm FEs — explain why). Add the `vce(robust)` option. Compare the DiD coefficient to the one from Task 3 and discuss whether and why it changed.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (treated vs. untreated) and `year`. Then use `twoway` to plot the two group means over time on the same graph, adding a vertical reference line at 1944 (the last pre-treatment year) with `xline(1944)`. Do the two groups move roughly in parallel before 1945?

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: The syntax for declaring a panel is `xtset panelvar timevar`. After declaring, `xtdescribe` gives you a compact summary of the panel structure — look for the phrase "strongly balanced" in the output.

- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: Use `gen treated = (company <= 5)` and `gen post = (year >= 1945)`. The parentheses evaluate a logical condition and return 1 if true, 0 if false — no `if` needed.

- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: The DiD coefficient on `did` captures the average change in `invest` for the treated group *relative* to the control group after the treatment year — this is the "treatment effect" under the parallel trends assumption.

- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: With firm fixed effects, any time-invariant characteristic of a company — including whether it is always treated or always control — is absorbed by the firm dummy. That is why `treated` cannot be separately identified. Think about what varies within a firm over time versus what is constant.
