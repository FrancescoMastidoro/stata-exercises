## Background
The `401k` dataset contains firm-level information on 401(k) pension plan participation, including match rates, plan age, employment size, and participation rates. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor and public economics, allowing researchers to estimate causal effects by comparing treated and control groups before and after a policy change.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the treatment variable** — Load the dataset and summarize `mrate`, `prate`, and `sole`. Then create a binary indicator called `high_match` equal to 1 if `mrate` is strictly greater than 0.5, and 0 otherwise. Report what share of firms qualify as high-match using `tabulate`.

2. **Create a time period indicator** — The variable `age` records plan age in years (ranging from 1 to 71). Create a binary variable called `mature` equal to 1 if `age` is greater than 10 (plans established more than 10 years ago), and 0 otherwise. Use `tabulate mature high_match` to examine the two-by-two cell counts that form the basis of a DiD design.

3. **Compute the DiD estimate by hand** — Using `tabstat prate, by(mature) statistics(mean)` separately for `high_match == 0` and `high_match == 1`, compute the four cell means manually. Then calculate the DiD estimate as `(mean_mature_highmatch − mean_notmature_highmatch) − (mean_mature_lowmatch − mean_notmature_lowmatch)` and write it as a comment in your do-file. Interpret the sign: does a higher match rate appear to boost participation more for mature plans?

4. **Estimate DiD via regression** — Run an OLS regression of `prate` on `high_match`, `mature`, and their interaction `high_match × mature`. Use `generate` to create the interaction term first, then run `regress`. Confirm that the coefficient on the interaction term matches (or closely approximates) your hand-calculated DiD estimate from Task 3. Also control for `ltotemp` to account for firm size.

5. *(Bonus)* **Robustness: continuous treatment** — Instead of the binary `high_match` indicator, use `mrate` directly as a continuous treatment variable. Re-run the regression from Task 4 replacing `high_match` and its interaction with `mrate` and `mrate × mature`. Compare the interaction coefficient to the binary-treatment result and discuss in comments whether binarizing the treatment variable appears to gain or lose meaningful variation.
