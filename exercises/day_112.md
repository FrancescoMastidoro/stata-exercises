# Day 112: Difference-in-Differences with Panel Data

**Dataset**: `webuse psidextract`
**Estimated time**: ~25 minutes

## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 person-period observations tracking workers across up to 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone of modern labor economics causal inference, exploiting variation in treatment timing or group membership across panels to recover credible treatment effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare the panel** — Load the dataset with `webuse psidextract`, then use `xtset` to declare it as a panel with individual identifier `id` and time variable `t`. Confirm the panel structure with `xtdescribe`.

2. **Construct a treatment indicator** — Union membership (`union`) varies over time and across workers. Create a new variable called `ever_union` equal to 1 if a worker is ever observed in a union across any of their periods, and 0 otherwise. Use `egen` with an appropriate function to compute this within each `id`. Inspect the distribution of `ever_union` with `tabulate`.

3. **Compute within-group mean wages** — Using `egen`, generate `mean_lwage_group` as the mean of `lwage` separately for the `ever_union` and `t` cells (i.e., group defined by `ever_union` × `t`). Then use `collapse` (on a copy or with `preserve`/`restore`) to produce a clean two-way table of mean log wages by `ever_union` and `t`, and display it. Examine whether the two groups trend similarly before any treatment — this is informal evidence for (or against) parallel trends.

4. **Estimate a DiD regression** — Run a two-way fixed effects regression of `lwage` on `union` (the time-varying treatment), absorbing individual fixed effects (`id`) and time fixed effects (`t`) using `xtreg` with the `fe` option and including `i.t` as a regressor. Interpret the coefficient on `union`: what does it estimate, and what identifying assumption underlies it?

5. *(Bonus)* **Add covariates and cluster standard errors** — Re-run the fixed effects DiD from Task 4, adding `occ`, `south`, `smsa`, `ms`, `ed`, and `wks` as additional controls, and clustering standard errors at the `id` level using `vce(cluster id)`. Compare the standard error on `union` to the unclustered version from Task 4 and explain why clustering matters in this panel setting.

- **Hint 1 (Task 2)**: `egen` has a function called `max()` that, when applied `by(id)`, will return the maximum value of a 0/1 variable for each individual — think about what the maximum of `union` over time tells you about a worker's union history.
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: To avoid permanently altering the dataset when using `collapse`, wrap your collapse and display commands inside `preserve` ... `restore`. The `by()` option of `collapse` lets you specify multiple grouping variables at once.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: With `xtreg, fe` you get individual fixed effects for free. To add time fixed effects on top, include `i.t` in the variable list just like any other regressor. The key DiD assumption here is that, absent union membership changes, treated and untreated workers' wages would have followed the same time path.
