## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, experience, and demographic information — a classic setting for evaluating how policy changes or life events affect labor market outcomes over time. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics because it controls for time-invariant individual heterogeneity and common time trends simultaneously, making it far more credible than a simple before/after comparison.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load `webuse regsmpl`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel with `idcode` as the individual identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm the structure with `xtdescribe` and report how many unique individuals and time periods are present.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `black_post80` equal to 1 if the observation is for a Black woman (`black == 1`) **and** the year is 1980 or later (`year >= 80`), and 0 otherwise. This will serve as the DiD interaction term, treating 1980 as a hypothetical policy cutoff.

3. **Run the baseline DiD regression** — Regress `ln_wage` on `black` (group indicator), a post-1980 dummy (`post80`, which you must also create), and your interaction `black_post80`. Use `xtreg, fe` to include individual fixed effects instead of `black` (since `black` is time-invariant and will be absorbed). Interpret the sign of the coefficient on `black_post80`.

4. **Add controls and cluster standard errors** — Augment the fixed-effects DiD regression from Task 3 by adding `ttl_exp`, `grade`, and `tenure` as controls (note: `tenure` and `grade` have missing values, so check how your sample size changes). Cluster standard errors at the `idcode` level using the `vce(cluster idcode)` option. Compare the standard error on `black_post80` with and without clustering.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption informally** — Restrict the sample to pre-1980 observations only (`year < 80`). Within this window, create year-specific interaction dummies between `black` and each unique year value (you may use `i.black##i.year` syntax in a pooled OLS regression with `reg`). Plot or inspect whether Black and non-Black women had systematically diverging `ln_wage` trends before the cutoff by checking whether these interaction coefficients are jointly significant using `testparm`.
