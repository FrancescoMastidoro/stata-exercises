## Background
The PSID extract contains 595 individuals observed over 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a workhorse identification strategy in labor economics that exploits variation in treatment timing across units to estimate causal effects while controlling for time-invariant unobservables.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure using `id` as the individual identifier and `t` as the time variable. Summarize `lwage`, `union`, `south`, and `fem` to get a sense of the data.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Generate a binary variable called `treated` that equals 1 if a worker is in a union (`union == 1`) **and** is in a southern state (`south == 1`), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `treated` by `t` to observe how the treatment group's size varies across periods.

3. **Estimate a pooled DiD** — Using `reg`, regress `lwage` on `union`, `south`, and your `treated` interaction variable, plus `t` as a linear time trend. Interpret the coefficient on `treated`: what does it represent in a DiD framework?

4. **Add fixed effects and cluster standard errors** — Re-estimate the model using `xtreg, fe` with individual fixed effects, including `t` as a time control. Cluster standard errors at the `id` level using the `vce(cluster id)` option. Compare the coefficient on `union` between the pooled OLS and the FE estimator — why might they differ?

5. *(Bonus)* **Test for parallel trends (informally)** — Restrict the sample to periods `t <= 4`. Generate a variable `t_trend` equal to `t` and interact it with `south`. Add this interaction to the pooled OLS regression from Task 3. If the DiD parallel trends assumption holds, what sign and significance would you expect on this pre-period interaction term, and why?

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse psidextract`, use `xtset id t` — Stata will confirm the panel is balanced. Then `sum lwage union south fem` gives you the basics.
- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: You can generate `treated` with `gen treated = (union == 1 & south == 1)`. Use `tab t treated` to see the cross-tabulation across periods.
- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: In a DiD setup, the coefficient on the interaction term (`treated`) captures the average treatment effect on the treated, over and above any level differences between groups and any common time trends.
- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: `xtreg lwage union south treated t, fe vce(cluster id)` is the right structure. Fixed effects absorb all time-invariant individual characteristics — think about what this means for variables like `south` and `fem` that don't vary over time within a person.
- **Hint 5 (Bonus)**: Under parallel trends, outcomes for treated and control groups should trend similarly *before* treatment. A statistically insignificant pre-trend coefficient is reassuring — it suggests the groups were on similar trajectories before any treatment occurred.
