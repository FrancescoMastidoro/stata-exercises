## Background
The Grunfeld dataset is a classic balanced panel of 10 large US firms observed annually from 1935 to 1954, containing firm investment, market value, and capital stock. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor and investment economics when right-hand-side variables are endogenous — here, market value and capital stock may both be correlated with unobserved firm-level shocks that also drive investment.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Run `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is strongly balanced, and summarize `invest`, `mvalue`, and `kstock`.

2. **OLS baseline** — Using `xtreg` with fixed effects (`fe`), regress `invest` on `mvalue` and `kstock`. Note the coefficients on both variables and save the result mentally (or using `estimates store`) as your baseline.

3. **Motivate the IV approach** — Run a simple correlation check: use `pwcorr mvalue kstock invest` to inspect relationships. Then explain in a comment in your `.do` file (using `*` lines) why `mvalue` might be endogenous — specifically, why unobserved firm-level productivity shocks could simultaneously raise investment and inflate market valuations.

4. **IV estimation with `xtivreg`** — Estimate the fixed-effects IV model using `xtivreg invest kstock (mvalue = L.mvalue L2.mvalue), fe`. Compare the IV coefficient on `mvalue` to the OLS fixed-effects estimate from Task 2. Then run `estat firststage` to inspect instrument strength and comment on whether the first-stage F-statistic suggests the instruments are relevant.

5. *(Bonus)* **Hausman test** — Store both the FE-OLS and FE-IV estimates using `estimates store`, then run a Hausman test (`hausman`) comparing them. Interpret whether the test result supports treating `mvalue` as endogenous, and discuss in comments what a rejection of the null hypothesis implies for your choice of estimator.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse grunfeld`, the correct `xtset` syntax places the unit identifier first and the time identifier second — check `help xtset` if you are unsure of the order. `xtdescribe` will then tell you the panel dimensions.

- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: In `xtivreg`, endogenous regressors go inside parentheses with the instruments listed after the `=` sign. `L.mvalue` refers to the one-period lag of market value — these are valid instruments if past values are correlated with current market value but not with current investment shocks. Use `estat firststage` immediately after estimation; a first-stage F-statistic above 10 is the conventional threshold for relevance.
