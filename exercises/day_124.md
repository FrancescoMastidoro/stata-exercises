## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed repeatedly from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, experience, and geographic indicators. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a workhorse method in labor economics for estimating the causal effect of a policy or treatment by comparing changes over time across treated and untreated groups.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load the dataset with `webuse regsmpl`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure (unit: `idcode`, time: `year`). Summarize `ln_wage`, `black`, `south`, and `year` to get a feel for the data. How many distinct years are in the sample?

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `post` equal to 1 for years strictly after 1977 (i.e., `year > 1977`) and 0 otherwise. This will serve as the "after" period in a DiD design where `black` is the "treated" group. Tabulate `post` against `year` to verify your coding is correct.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term** — Generate a variable `did` equal to the product of `black` and `post`. This is the classic interaction term whose coefficient captures the DiD estimate. Summarize `black`, `post`, and `did` in a single `summarize` call and briefly interpret what the mean of `did` tells you about the share of treated-period observations.

4. **Estimate the DiD model** — Run an OLS regression of `ln_wage` on `black`, `post`, and `did`, using `vce(cluster idcode)` to account for within-individual correlation. Then run a second regression that additionally controls for `ttl_exp` and `grade` (which has 2 missing values — note how the sample size changes). Compare the DiD coefficient across the two specifications and interpret its sign and magnitude.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add individual fixed effects** — Estimate the DiD model using `xtreg ln_wage post did ttl_exp, fe vce(cluster idcode)`. Note that `black` drops out — explain in a comment why this happens in a fixed-effects estimator. Compare the `did` coefficient to your OLS estimates from Task 4 and discuss what absorbing individual fixed effects does to the estimate.
