## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains annual investment data for 10 large U.S. firms from 1935 to 1954, making it a classic panel used to study corporate investment behavior. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is one of the most widely used quasi-experimental strategies in labor and applied economics, and practicing it on a clean panel helps you build intuition before applying it to messier real-world data.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `company` as the unit identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm that the panel is strongly balanced by inspecting how many observations exist per company using `xtdescribe` or `tabulate company`.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary "post" variable equal to 1 for years 1945 and later (i.e., the post-war period), and 0 otherwise. Then create a binary "treated" variable equal to 1 for companies 1–5 and 0 for companies 6–10. Verify both variables with `tabulate`.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term** — Generate the interaction variable `did = treated * post`. This is the variable whose coefficient will capture the DiD estimator. Use `tabulate treated post` to confirm that your 2×2 treatment-timing cell structure looks correct.

4. **Run the DiD regression and interpret** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using `xtreg` with company fixed effects (`fe`). Report what the coefficient on `did` tells you about the average treatment effect on investment for the treated firms in the post-war period, compared to control firms.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add a continuous control and cluster standard errors** — Re-run the fixed-effects DiD regression from Task 4, adding `mvalue` and `kstock` as controls. Cluster standard errors at the `company` level using the `vce(cluster company)` option. Compare the standard error on `did` to the unclustered version and explain why clustering matters in panel DiD settings.
