## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed across survey years from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, experience, and demographic variables across thousands of individuals. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects of policies by comparing treated and control groups before and after an intervention.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel structure** — Load the dataset with `webuse regsmpl`, then declare it as a panel using `xtset`. Confirm the panel identifier and time variable, and report how many unique individuals and time periods exist using `xtsum` on `ln_wage`.

2. **Construct a treatment group indicator** — Create a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 if an individual is `black` (use the `black` variable, which has no missing values). Then create a binary `post` variable equal to 1 if `year` is greater than or equal to 80 (i.e., 1980 onward). Display the frequency distribution of `post` using `tabulate`.

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Construct an interaction term `did = treated * post` manually, then regress `ln_wage` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Interpret the coefficient on `did` as your DiD estimate. Use `vce(cluster idcode)` for standard errors that account for within-individual correlation.

4. **Extend to a two-way fixed effects DiD** — Re-run the DiD using `xtreg` with both individual fixed effects (`fe`) and include `post` and `did` in the model. Then separately run `regress ln_wage did i.year` adding year fixed effects and `ttl_exp` as a control — compare how the DiD estimate changes when you add time fixed effects and covariates. Use `vce(cluster idcode)` throughout.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends check** — Create year-specific interaction terms between `treated` and indicators for each survey year (i.e., `treated × i.year`) and regress `ln_wage` on these interactions plus individual and year fixed effects using `xtreg, fe`. Plot or inspect whether the treatment-year coefficients are close to zero in the pre-1980 period, which is the empirical check for the parallel trends assumption.

Notice whether including `ttl_exp` shifts your DiD estimate — this tells you whether experience accumulation was trending differently across groups.
