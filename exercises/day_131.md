# Day 131: Difference-in-Differences with Panel Data

## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations on 595 workers observed across up to 7 time periods, with information on wages, employment characteristics, and demographics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects by comparing changes over time across groups, and panel data like this one are ideal for practicing it.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure (unit: `id`, time: `t`). Confirm the declaration with `xtdescribe` and note how many periods each individual is observed.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary treatment variable `treated` equal to 1 if a worker is in a union (`union == 1`) *and* in a blue-collar occupation (`occ == 1`), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `treated` to check its distribution.

3. **Construct a post-period dummy** — Create a variable `post` equal to 1 if `t >= 5` (the latter half of the panel) and 0 otherwise. Then generate the DiD interaction term `did = treated * post`. Summarize all three new variables.

4. **Estimate the DiD model** — Regress `lwage` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`, controlling for `ed`, `exp`, `south`, and `smsa`. Use `xtreg, fe` (fixed effects estimator) to absorb individual-level unobservables. Interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it estimate, and what is its sign and magnitude?

5. *(Bonus)* **Check the parallel trends assumption informally** — Collapse the data to period-level cell means of `lwage` by `treated` and `t`. Plot (or tabulate) mean wages by group across all 7 periods to visually inspect whether the two groups were on similar wage trajectories *before* `t = 5`. Comment on what you observe.

## Hints

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: `xtset` requires you to specify the panel identifier first and the time variable second — check the variable reference to confirm which variables serve these roles in `psidextract`, then use `xtdescribe` to see the balance of the panel.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: Remember that an interaction term in DiD is simply the product of two binary variables; you can generate it with `gen did = treated * post` after creating both components separately.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: With `xtreg, fe`, Stata absorbs the within-individual mean, so time-invariant variables like `ed` or `blk` will be dropped automatically — this is expected behavior, not an error. The coefficient on `did` is your causal estimate of interest.

- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: The `collapse` command with `(mean) lwage` grouped by `treated t` will give you the period-by-group means you need; you can then use `list` or `twoway line` to inspect trends.
