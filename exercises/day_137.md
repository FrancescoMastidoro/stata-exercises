## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations tracking 595 individuals across 7 time periods, with wages, employment characteristics, and demographic variables recorded at each wave. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects of policy changes by comparing treated and control groups before and after an intervention.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel structure** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then declare the panel structure using `xtset`. Verify the structure by running `xtdescribe` and confirm that `id` ranges from 1 to 595 and `t` runs from 1 to 7.

2. **Construct a DiD treatment indicator** — Define "treated" individuals as those in a union (`union == 1`) and "post" periods as `t >= 4`. Create two new variables: `post` (equal to 1 if `t >= 4`, 0 otherwise) and `treated_post` (the interaction of `union` and `post`). Tabulate `post` and cross-tabulate `union` by `post` to understand the composition of your quasi-experiment.

3. **Estimate a basic DiD regression** — Regress `lwage` on `union`, `post`, and `treated_post` using OLS (`regress`). Interpret the coefficient on `treated_post` as the DiD estimate of the union wage premium in post periods relative to pre periods. Note any concerns about this specification.

4. **Add individual fixed effects and controls** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg lwage union post treated_post exp wks occ south smsa, fe`. Compare the coefficient on `treated_post` from the pooled OLS and the fixed-effects specifications. Explain in a comment why the fixed-effects estimate may differ and what individual fixed effects absorb in this context.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption graphically** — Using `egen`, compute the mean of `lwage` by `t` separately for union and non-union workers. Then use `twoway` to plot both mean wage series over time (one line per group). Add a vertical reference line at `t == 4` using `xline(4)`. Discuss in comments whether the pre-period trends look approximately parallel.
