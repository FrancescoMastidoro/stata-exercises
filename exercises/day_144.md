## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply decisions, including women's education, their partner's education, number of kids, other household income, female income, and an indicator for whether the woman works. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor economics for recovering causal effects when a key regressor is endogenous — for example, when education and income are simultaneously determined.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Identify which variables are continuous and which are binary. Note the ranges of `fem_educ`, `male_educ`, `other_inc`, and `fem_inc` from the summary output.

2. **OLS baseline** — Regress `fem_inc` on `fem_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc` using OLS. Report the coefficient on `fem_educ` and its standard error. Briefly comment (in a code comment) on why OLS estimates of the return to female education may be biased.

3. **First-stage regression** — Run a regression of `fem_educ` on your proposed instrument `male_educ`, plus the controls `kids` and `other_inc`. Check whether `male_educ` is a strong predictor of `fem_educ` by examining its t-statistic and the overall F-statistic of the regression.

4. **IV estimation with `ivregress`** — Estimate the effect of `fem_educ` on `fem_inc` using `male_educ` as an instrument, controlling for `kids` and `other_inc`. Use the 2SLS estimator. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_educ` to the OLS coefficient from Task 2 — does endogeneity appear to matter here?

5. *(Bonus)* **Hausman-style endogeneity test and weak instrument check** — After your `ivregress 2sls` command, run `estat endogenous` to formally test whether `fem_educ` is endogenous, and `estat firststage` to obtain the first-stage F-statistic and compare it against the conventional threshold of 10. Interpret both test results in a comment.

Map your variables carefully: the endogenous regressor goes inside the parentheses on the left of `=`, and the instrument goes on the right.
