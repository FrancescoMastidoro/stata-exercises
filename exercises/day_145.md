## Background
The Mroz (1987) PSID dataset is a classic resource in labor economics, containing detailed information on married women's labor supply, wages, education, and family background. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is essential when a key regressor — such as education — is endogenous due to omitted ability bias or reverse causality, and finding a valid instrument is one of the central challenges in applied microeconometrics.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the key variables** — Load the dataset and summarize `wwage76`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `k618`. Note the ranges and think about which variables might be correlated with wages but potentially endogenous.

2. **Run a baseline OLS regression** — Regress `wwage76` (wife's 1976 hourly wage) on `wedyrs` (wife's education), `wexper` (work experience), `kl6` (kids under 6), and `k618` (kids 6–18). Report and interpret the coefficient on `wedyrs`.

3. **Instrument wife's education** — Use `wmedyrs` (wife's mother's education) and `wfedyrs` (wife's father's education) as instruments for `wedyrs`. Run a 2SLS regression using `ivregress 2sls` with the same controls as in Task 2. Report how the coefficient on `wedyrs` changes relative to OLS.

4. **Check instrument relevance with a first-stage F-test** — After your `ivregress` command, use `estat firststage` to obtain the first-stage F-statistic. Discuss whether your instruments satisfy the relevance condition using the conventional threshold of F > 10.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test for endogeneity** — Use `estat endogenous` after your IV regression to test whether `wedyrs` is statistically endogenous. Interpret the resulting p-value: does OLS appear to produce inconsistent estimates here, or is the endogeneity concern mild in this sample?

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: In `ivregress 2sls`, the syntax places endogenous regressors and their instruments inside parentheses: `ivregress 2sls y x1 x2 (endog_var = instrument1 instrument2)`. Make sure your exogenous controls appear *outside* the parentheses.
- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: `estat firststage` must be run *immediately after* `ivregress` and reports the partial F-statistic for the excluded instruments in the first stage — this is what you compare against the rule-of-thumb threshold, not the overall model F.
