## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains annual investment, market value, and capital stock data for 10 large U.S. firms observed from 1935 to 1954 — a classic panel used in econometrics textbooks. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a workhorse method in labor and industrial economics for estimating causal effects when randomization is not possible, exploiting variation across units and time.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel with `company` as the unit identifier and `year` as the time variable. Verify the panel is balanced by inspecting the output.

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — Generate a binary variable called `post` that equals 1 for years strictly after 1944 and 0 otherwise. Then create a binary variable called `treated` that equals 1 for companies with `company` ≤ 5 and 0 for companies with `company` > 5. Tabulate both variables to confirm their distributions.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run a basic DiD regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did` equal to `treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Report and interpret the coefficient on `did` — what does it represent in a DiD framework?

4. **Upgrade to a two-way fixed effects (TWFE) specification** — Re-run the DiD regression using `xtreg` with firm fixed effects (`fe`) and add `i.year` to absorb time fixed effects. Compare the coefficient on `did` to the one from Task 3. Why might the estimates differ?

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends visual check** — Using `egen`, compute the mean of `invest` by `year` and `treated` group. Then use `twoway` to plot the average investment over time for treated and control firms on the same graph (using two separate `line` plots). Do the pre-1945 trends look parallel?

## Hints
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal hints (try first!)</summary>

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: The syntax for declaring a panel is `xtset panelvar timevar`. Look at the output line that says "balanced" or "unbalanced" to confirm the panel structure — you can also cross-check with `xtdescribe`.
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: In a standard DiD setup, the coefficient on the interaction term (`treated × post`) is the causal estimate of interest — it captures the change in the outcome for the treated group *relative* to the control group, *relative* to the pre-period change. Think about what `treated` and `post` alone are controlling for.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: When using `xtreg, fe`, Stata automatically absorbs firm-level fixed effects. To additionally control for year fixed effects, include `i.year` in the regressor list. You do not need to manually include `treated` as a separate regressor when firm FE are already in the model — think about why.
- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: `egen mean_invest = mean(invest), by(year treated)` will give you group-year averages. To plot two lines on the same graph, use `twoway (line mean_invest year if treated==1) (line mean_invest year if treated==0)`.
