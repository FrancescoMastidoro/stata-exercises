## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply decisions, including women's and husbands' education, number of children, non-labor income, and female earnings. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor economics for recovering causal effects when a regressor is endogenous — here, you will use husband's education as an instrument for female income to study its effect on female labor force participation.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Identify which variable captures female labor force participation (binary) and which variables measure income and education for both spouses. Note the ranges of `fem_inc`, `other_inc`, `fem_educ`, and `male_educ`.

2. **OLS baseline** — Regress `fem_work` on `fem_inc`, `kids`, `other_inc`, and `fem_educ` using OLS. Report the coefficient on `fem_inc` and interpret its sign: does higher female income increase or decrease the probability of working, and does this direction make intuitive sense in a labor supply context?

3. **First-stage regression** — Run the first-stage regression of `fem_inc` on the proposed instrument `male_educ` plus the controls `kids`, `other_inc`, and `fem_educ`. Check whether `male_educ` is a relevant instrument by examining its t-statistic and the overall F-statistic of the first stage. A strong instrument typically has an F-statistic above 10.

4. **IV estimation with `ivregress`** — Estimate the structural equation for `fem_work` using 2SLS: regress `fem_work` on `fem_inc`, `kids`, `other_inc`, and `fem_educ`, instrumenting `fem_inc` with `male_educ`. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_inc` with the OLS estimate from Task 2 and discuss whether the direction or magnitude changes substantially — what does this suggest about endogeneity bias in OLS?

5. *(Bonus)* **Hausman-style endogeneity test** — After your `ivregress 2sls` command, run `estat endogenous` to formally test whether `fem_inc` is endogenous. State the null hypothesis of this test, report the p-value, and explain what conclusion you draw about whether IV estimation is necessary here. Then re-estimate using `ivregress liml` and compare the LIML coefficient on `fem_inc` to the 2SLS estimate.
