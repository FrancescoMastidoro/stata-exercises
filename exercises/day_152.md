## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply decisions, including own and spousal education, number of children, and income variables. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics when regressors like own income are correlated with unobserved determinants of labor force participation, and a valid instrument (one that shifts the endogenous variable but has no direct effect on the outcome) is needed to recover causal estimates.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore and describe the data** — Load the dataset and use `describe` and `summarize` to familiarise yourself with all six variables. Note the range of `fem_educ`, `male_educ`, `kids`, `other_inc`, `fem_inc`, and `fem_work`. Which variables would you expect to be correlated with female labor supply (`fem_work`)?

2. **Estimate an OLS baseline** — Regress `fem_work` on `fem_inc`, `kids`, and `other_inc` using OLS. Interpret the sign and statistical significance of the coefficient on `fem_inc`. Why might this OLS estimate be biased if `fem_inc` is endogenous?

3. **Run a first-stage regression** — Regress `fem_inc` on the proposed instrument `male_educ`, plus the controls `kids` and `other_inc`. Report the F-statistic for the instrument and discuss whether `male_educ` appears to be a strong instrument (the conventional threshold is F > 10).

4. **Estimate the IV (2SLS) model** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to instrument `fem_inc` with `male_educ`, keeping `kids` and `other_inc` as exogenous controls. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_inc` to the OLS estimate from Task 2, and use `estat firststage` immediately after to retrieve the first-stage F-statistic within the IV framework.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test endogeneity formally** — After the `ivregress` command, run `estat endogenous` to conduct a Hausman-type test of whether `fem_inc` is indeed endogenous. State the null hypothesis, report the p-value, and explain what conclusion you draw about whether IV was necessary here.
