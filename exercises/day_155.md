## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations on 595 workers observed over 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, industry, occupation, and demographic characteristics. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, allowing researchers to estimate causal effects by comparing changes across treated and control groups over time.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure using `id` and `t`. Confirm the panel is balanced by running `xtdescribe`. How many individuals are observed in all 7 periods?

2. **Define a treatment indicator** — Create a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 if a worker is in a union (`union == 1`) **and** is in period `t >= 4`. This will serve as your DiD treatment dummy (i.e., "union status switched on from period 4 onward"). Tabulate `treated` by `t` to verify the cell counts look sensible.

3. **Estimate a basic DiD regression** — Regress `lwage` on `union` (the "group" indicator), a dummy for `t >= 4` (the "post" indicator), and their interaction term. The interaction coefficient is your DiD estimate. Interpret the sign and magnitude of the coefficient on the interaction term.

4. **Add controls and fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg` with individual fixed effects (`fe`). Include `ed`, `exp`, `wks`, `south`, and `smsa` as time-varying controls, and use `i.t` to absorb period fixed effects. Compare the DiD estimate to the simple OLS estimate from Task 3 — does controlling for unobserved individual heterogeneity change the result, and in which direction?

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends check** — To provide informal evidence for the parallel trends assumption, create a period-by-period interaction between `union` and each period dummy `i.t`, and plot (using `coefplot` if available, or `margins` + `marginsplot`) the estimated wage gap between union and non-union workers across all 7 periods. Do the pre-period gaps look stable before `t = 4`?

- **Hint 1 (Task 2)**: You can create `treated` with a single `gen` statement combining two conditions with `&`. Use `table treated t` or `tab treated t` to inspect the distribution across periods.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: To construct the interaction manually, first generate `post = (t >= 4)`, then generate `union_post = union * post`. Your regression will have three regressors: `union`, `post`, and `union_post` — the last one is the DiD coefficient.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: The syntax for a two-way fixed effects model is `xtreg lwage union post union_post ed exp wks south smsa i.t, fe`. Note that `union` (if it does not vary within individuals) may be absorbed — check whether Stata drops it and think about why.
