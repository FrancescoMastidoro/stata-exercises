## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains firm-level investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. corporations observed annually from 1935 to 1954 — a classic balanced panel used in econometrics teaching. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor and applied economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing across units to estimate causal effects while controlling for time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel structure** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset` with `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable, then run `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company` ≤ 5 (the "treated group") and 0 otherwise. Then create a post-period indicator `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 for years before 1945. Verify your definitions with `tabulate`.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run a basic DiD regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Report and interpret the coefficient on `did` — what does it represent in a DiD framework?

4. **Upgrade to a two-way fixed effects estimator** — Estimate the same DiD model using `xtreg` with firm fixed effects (`fe`) and add `i.year` to absorb time fixed effects. Compare the coefficient on `did` from this specification to the one obtained in Task 3, and explain in a comment why the two estimates differ.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends check** — Create a set of year-by-year interaction terms between `treated` and each year dummy (omitting one pre-treatment year as the reference), run a regression of `invest` on these interactions plus year and company fixed effects, and plot or inspect the pre-treatment coefficients to assess whether the parallel trends assumption appears plausible visually.
