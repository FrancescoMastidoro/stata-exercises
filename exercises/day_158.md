# Day 158: Difference-in-Differences with Panel Data

**Dataset**: `webuse psidextract`

## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations on workers observed across up to 7 time periods, with variables on wages, union status, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, allowing you to estimate causal effects by comparing changes over time between a treated and an untreated group.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then declare it as a panel using `xtset` with `id` as the panel variable and `t` as the time variable. Summarize `lwage`, `union`, `south`, and `fem` to get a sense of the data.

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — In this exercise, treat time periods `t >= 4` as the "post" period. Generate a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `t >= 4` and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `t` and `post` together to verify your indicator is correct.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term** — Define `union` as the "treatment" group (workers who are in a union at any point). Generate a person-level variable `ever_union` equal to 1 if a worker's average `union` status across all their observations is greater than 0 (i.e., they were ever unionized). Then create the DiD interaction term `did = ever_union * post`.

4. **Estimate the DiD regression** — Run an OLS regression of `lwage` on `ever_union`, `post`, and `did`, controlling for `ed`, `fem`, `blk`, and `south`. Interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it represent in a labor economics context? Then re-run the same specification adding individual fixed effects using `xtreg, fe` and compare the two estimates.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `lwage` separately by `t` and `ever_union`. Then use `twoway line` to plot the average log wage over time for union and non-union workers on the same graph. Does the pre-period trend (t = 1 to 3) look parallel between the two groups?
