## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, capturing variables such as female income, education, number of kids, and partner characteristics. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics when a regressor — like education or income — is correlated with unobserved factors that also affect the outcome, causing OLS to be biased and inconsistent.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Note the ranges and meaning of each variable. Then estimate a simple OLS regression of `fem_inc` on `fem_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc`. Report the coefficient on `fem_educ` and explain in a comment what bias concern might arise.

2. **Check instrument relevance** — Regress `fem_educ` on your proposed instrument `male_educ` (controlling for `kids` and `other_inc`). Report the coefficient on `male_educ` and its t-statistic. Explain in a comment whether the first-stage relationship appears strong.

3. **Run IV via `ivregress 2sls`** — Instrument `fem_educ` with `male_educ`, controlling for `kids` and `other_inc`, with `fem_inc` as the dependent variable. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_educ` to the OLS estimate from Task 1. Does the direction or magnitude change noticeably?

4. **Test instrument validity** — After the `ivregress` command, use `estat firststage` to retrieve first-stage statistics. Then use `estat endogtest` to test whether `fem_educ` is actually endogenous. Interpret the p-value: does the test suggest OLS would have been acceptable, or is IV warranted?

5. *(Bonus)* **Two instruments, overidentification** — Re-run `ivregress 2sls` instrumenting `fem_educ` with both `male_educ` and `male_educ`-squared (create `male_educ2 = male_educ^2` first). Then run `estat overid` to perform the Sargan–Hansen overidentification test. Interpret the result: do both instruments appear valid?
