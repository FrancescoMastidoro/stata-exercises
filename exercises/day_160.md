## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset contains panel observations of NLS women tracked from 1968 to 1988, recording wages, education, experience, and geographic identifiers across multiple years. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing across groups to isolate causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset with `webuse regsmpl`. Declare the panel using `xtset`, then summarize `ln_wage`, `black`, `south`, and `ttl_exp`. Report the number of unique individuals and the time span covered by `year`.

2. **Define a post-period indicator** — Create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `year` is greater than or equal to 78 (i.e., 1978 onward), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `post` against `year` to verify the split is correct.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term** — Use `black` as the "treatment" group indicator (workers who are Black). Generate an interaction variable called `did` equal to `black × post`. Tabulate `black` and `post` jointly, then report how many observations fall in each of the four cells.

4. **Run the DiD regression and interpret** — Regress `ln_wage` on `black`, `post`, and `did`, adding `ttl_exp` as a control and clustering standard errors by `idcode`. Write out what the coefficient on `did` represents in the context of the parallel trends assumption, and note its sign and significance.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add year fixed effects** — Re-run the DiD regression from Task 4 but replace the single `post` dummy with a full set of year fixed effects using `i.year`, still clustering by `idcode`. Compare the coefficient on `did` before and after adding year fixed effects, and discuss what absorbing year-level shocks does to the estimate and its precision.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse regsmpl`, use `xtset idcode year` to declare the panel. `codebook year` and `distinct idcode` (or `tabulate year`) will help you describe the structure without `distinct` if unavailable — `quietly tab idcode` followed by `display r(r)` works too.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: Generating the interaction is as simple as `gen did = black * post`. Remember `black` has no missing values, but `post` is a variable you just created — double-check with `tab black post` that the four cells make sense before proceeding to regression.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: The syntax for clustered standard errors in `regress` is `vce(cluster idcode)`. The coefficient on `did` captures the *differential change* in log wages for Black women relative to non-Black women, moving from pre- to post-1978 — this is the classic 2×2 DiD estimand.

- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: Replacing `post` with `i.year` in the regression formula absorbs all time-period shocks common to everyone. Watch how the standard error on `did` changes relative to Task 4, and think about whether the year fixed effects are absorbing variation that was previously attributed to the interaction.
