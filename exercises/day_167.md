## Background
The Grunfeld dataset tracks investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. firms over 20 years (1935–1954), making it a classic panel dataset in econometrics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor and applied economics for estimating causal effects when treatment varies across units and over time.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset and declare it as a panel with `company` as the unit identifier and `year` as the time variable. Then run `xtdescribe` to verify the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company` ≤ 5 (the "treated" group) and 0 otherwise. Then create a post-period indicator `post` equal to 1 for years after 1944 (i.e., `year` > 1944) and 0 otherwise. Tabulate both variables to confirm their distribution across the 200 observations.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run a basic DiD regression** — Generate the interaction `did = treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using OLS (`regress`). Report and interpret the coefficient on `did` — this is your DiD estimate of the average treatment effect on investment.

4. **Improve the specification with two-way fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg` with fixed effects (`fe`) to absorb company-level unobserved heterogeneity, and add `year` dummies (use `i.year`) to control for common time shocks. Compare the DiD estimate from this two-way fixed effects model to the simple OLS result from Task 3 — does the estimate change substantially?

5. *(Bonus)* **Visualise the parallel trends assumption** — Compute the average value of `invest` by `year` and `treated` group using `collapse` (preserve your data first with `preserve`/`restore`). Then use `twoway` to plot both group trends over time on the same graph, using different line patterns or colors for treated and control. Does the pre-1945 period look consistent with parallel trends?

## Hints

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: The command to declare panel structure in Stata is `xtset`; it takes two arguments — the panel identifier and the time identifier, in that order.
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: You can generate the interaction manually with `gen did = treated * post`, or equivalently include `c.treated#c.post` directly in the regression formula — both are valid, but the manual approach makes the coefficient label immediately obvious.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: With `xtreg, fe`, Stata automatically absorbs the company fixed effects; you still need to add time fixed effects explicitly by including `i.year` on the right-hand side of the regression.
- **Hint 4 (Bonus)**: After `preserve`, use `collapse (mean) invest, by(year treated)` to get group-year averages. You can then use `twoway (line invest year if treated==1) (line invest year if treated==0)` as a starting point for your plot.
