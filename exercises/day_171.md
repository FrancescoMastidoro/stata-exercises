## Background
The Mroz 1987 dataset is a classic panel from the PSID tracking married women's labor market decisions, containing rich information on wages, education, family structure, and non-labor income. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics whenever a regressor like education or experience is correlated with unobserved ability or motivation, and a valid instrument allows us to recover a causal estimate.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the key variables** — Load the dataset and run `summarize` on `wwage76`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `nwinc`. Then use `tabulate lfp` to understand how many wives participate in the labor force. Report the mean of `wedyrs` and the fraction of wives working.

2. **OLS baseline** — Among wives who are in the labor force (`lfp == 1`), regress `wwage76` on `wedyrs`, `wexper`, and `kl6`. Interpret the coefficient on `wedyrs`: what does it imply about the return to an additional year of education? Note any concerns about endogeneity.

3. **First-stage regression** — Still restricting to `lfp == 1`, regress `wedyrs` on the proposed instruments `wmedyrs` (wife's mother's years of education) and `wfedyrs` (wife's father's years of education), plus the controls `wexper` and `kl6`. Assess instrument relevance by examining the F-statistic and the sign and significance of the instrument coefficients. Is the first stage strong?

4. **IV estimation with `ivregress`** — Using the same sample (`lfp == 1`), estimate the IV (2SLS) model where `wwage76` is the dependent variable, `wedyrs` is instrumented by `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs`, and `wexper` and `kl6` are included as exogenous controls. Compare the IV coefficient on `wedyrs` to the OLS estimate from Task 2. Is it larger or smaller? What might explain the difference?

5. *(Bonus)* **Overidentification test** — After the `ivregress 2sls` command, run `estat overid` to perform the Sargan–Hansen overidentification test. State the null hypothesis of the test, report the p-value, and explain what you would conclude about the joint validity of `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs` as instruments. Then re-estimate using only `wmedyrs` as the single instrument and compare results.

- **Hint 1 (Task 2)**: To restrict estimation to labor-force participants only, you can either use `if lfp == 1` at the end of your `regress` command, or `keep if lfp == 1` after saving the full dataset — using the `if` qualifier is cleaner and safer here.

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: After `regress wedyrs wmedyrs wfedyrs wexper kl6 if lfp==1`, Stata automatically reports an F-statistic at the bottom of the output. A common rule of thumb is that F > 10 suggests instruments are not weak — check whether yours clears this bar.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: The syntax for 2SLS in Stata follows the pattern `ivregress 2sls depvar (endogenous = instruments) exogenous_controls`. The endogenous regressor and its instruments go inside the parentheses, while truly exogenous controls sit outside.

- **Hint 4 (Bonus)**: `estat overid` is only available after `ivregress` and only when the model is overidentified (more instruments than endogenous regressors). Think carefully about what "failure to reject" the null means for your instruments' validity — and what the test *cannot* tell you.
