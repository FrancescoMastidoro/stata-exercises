## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains firm-level investment data for 10 US companies across 20 years (1935–1954), making it a classic panel used to study investment behavior. Difference-in-differences (DiD) exploits variation across units and time to identify causal effects, and practicing it on a well-known panel helps build intuition before applying the method to real policy questions in labor economics.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the data** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `company` as the unit identifier and `year` as the time variable, then run `xtdescribe` to verify the panel is balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1–5 and 0 for companies 6–10. Then create a post-period indicator `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 otherwise. Finally, generate the interaction term `did = treated * post`, which is the DiD regressor of interest.

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using OLS (`regress`). Report the coefficient on `did` and interpret it in words: what does it measure in this context?

4. **Add firm fixed effects and cluster standard errors** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg, fe` to absorb firm fixed effects (which subsumes `treated`), include `post` and `did` as regressors, and cluster standard errors at the `company` level. Compare the coefficient on `did` and its standard error to those from Task 3 — explain why the estimates may differ.

5. *(Bonus)* **Inspect the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (treated vs. control) and `year`. Then plot the two group means over time with `twoway line`, adding a vertical reference line at 1945 using `xline(1945)`. Discuss informally whether the pre-treatment trends look parallel.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After loading the data, use `xtset company year` to declare the panel structure — then `xtdescribe` will tell you whether every company × year cell is observed.

- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: You can create `treated` with `gen treated = (company <= 5)` — Stata evaluates the Boolean expression and stores 1 or 0 automatically. A similar one-liner works for `post`.

- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: In a standard DiD setup, the coefficient on the interaction term `did` captures the average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) — think about what "treated × post" actually picks up relative to the other two terms in the regression.

- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: With `xtreg, fe`, firm-level time-invariant variables (like `treated`) are collinear with the fixed effects and will be dropped — this is expected. For clustering, add `vce(cluster company)` as an option; with only 10 clusters, note that this may produce wider confidence intervals.

- **Hint 5 (Bonus)**: Before collapsing, generate a group variable equal to `treated` (which is already 0/1). After `collapse (mean) invest, by(treated year)`, use `twoway (line invest year if treated==1) (line invest year if treated==0)` to overlay both series.
