# Day 176: Instrumental Variables (IV) with Two-Stage Least Squares

**Dataset**: `webuse mroz87`
**Estimated time**: ~25 minutes

## Background
The Mroz 1987 PSID extract contains detailed information on married women's labor supply, wages, education, and family characteristics in 1975–1976. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics when a regressor like education is plausibly endogenous — for instance, because unobserved ability simultaneously drives both schooling and wages.

## Your tasks

1. **OLS baseline** — Load `webuse mroz87`. Among wives who worked in 1975 (i.e., `wwage75 > 0`), regress log hourly wage (`wwage75`, after taking the log with `gen`) on wife's education (`wedyrs`), experience (`wexper`), and SMSA status (`smsa`). Report and note the OLS coefficient on `wedyrs`.

2. **First-stage regression** — Still restricting to working wives, regress `wedyrs` on the proposed instruments `wmedyrs` (wife's mother's years of education) and `wfedyrs` (wife's father's years of education), plus the same controls (`wexper`, `smsa`). Comment on whether the instruments appear relevant by checking the F-statistic for joint significance of the two instruments.

3. **IV estimation via `ivregress 2sls`** — Run a 2SLS regression of log wage on `wexper` and `smsa` (exogenous), instrumenting `wedyrs` with `wmedyrs` and `wfedyrs`. Compare the IV coefficient on `wedyrs` to the OLS estimate from Task 1 — is the IV estimate larger or smaller, and what might explain the difference?

4. **Instrument relevance test** — After the `ivregress` command, use `estat firststage` to obtain first-stage F-statistics. Based on the conventional threshold (F > 10), evaluate whether the instruments are strong or weak. Then use `estat overid` to run a test of overidentifying restrictions and interpret what a rejection would imply in this context.

5. *(Bonus)* **Heteroskedasticity-robust IV** — Re-run the `ivregress 2sls` model from Task 3 using the `vce(robust)` option. Compare the standard error on `wedyrs` to the non-robust version. Then test whether `wedyrs` is actually endogenous using `estat endogenous` and state the null hypothesis of the test.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: You need to create a log wage variable first — use `gen lwwage75 = log(wwage75)` and remember to restrict the sample with `if wwage75 > 0` in every regression, since `wwage75` equals zero for non-working wives.
- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: To test joint significance of only the instruments in the first stage, use `test wmedyrs wfedyrs` immediately after the `regress` command — this gives an F-statistic for the two instruments together.
- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: The syntax for `ivregress 2sls` places exogenous regressors and the instrument list inside parentheses: `ivregress 2sls depvar (endogenous = instruments) exogenous_controls, ...` — make sure you know which variables go where.
- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: `estat firststage` and `estat overid` are post-estimation commands that only work directly after `ivregress`. The overidentification test (Sargan-Hansen) requires the model to be *overidentified* — think about how many instruments versus endogenous regressors you have here.
