## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, capturing women's earnings, education, their husband's education, and the number of children. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of labor economics, allowing researchers to recover causal effects when regressors are correlated with unobserved factors — a near-universal problem in wage and labor supply regressions.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Note the ranges of `fem_inc`, `fem_educ`, `male_educ`, and `kids`. Then regress `fem_inc` on `fem_educ` using OLS and save the coefficient on `fem_educ` mentally for later comparison.

2. **First-stage regression** — Run an OLS regression of `fem_educ` on `male_educ` and `kids`. Report the coefficients and comment on whether `male_educ` appears to be a relevant predictor of `fem_educ` (i.e., is the coefficient statistically significant?).

3. **IV estimation via `ivregress`** — Use `ivregress 2sls fem_inc (fem_educ = male_educ) kids` to estimate the causal effect of female education on female income, instrumenting for `fem_educ` with `male_educ` while controlling for `kids`. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_educ` to the OLS estimate from Task 1 and describe any difference in magnitude or sign.

4. **Test instrument relevance and exogeneity** — After your `ivregress` command, run `estat firststage` to retrieve the first-stage F-statistic and assess whether the instrument is strong (rule of thumb: F > 10). Then add a second instrument by also using `other_inc` alongside `male_educ`, re-run `ivregress 2sls`, and use `estat overid` to conduct a Sargan–Hansen test of overidentifying restrictions. Interpret the p-value: does it support instrument exogeneity?

5. *(Bonus)* **Hausman test for endogeneity** — Using the `ivregress` framework, run `estat endogenous` after your just-identified IV model (with only `male_educ` as the instrument) to formally test whether `fem_educ` is endogenous. State the null hypothesis, report the test statistic and p-value, and explain what the result implies for whether OLS or IV is the preferred estimator in this context.

## Hints

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: The syntax for `ivregress 2sls` is `ivregress 2sls depvar (endogenous_var = instrument) controls`. The parentheses separate endogenous regressors from their instruments; exogenous controls go outside the parentheses.

- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: `estat firststage`, `estat overid`, and `estat endogenous` are post-estimation commands that must be typed immediately after `ivregress`. To use `estat overid`, your model must be overidentified — meaning you need more instruments than endogenous regressors (here: two instruments, one endogenous variable). The Sargan–Hansen test's null hypothesis is that all instruments are exogenous; a high p-value (> 0.05) means you *fail to reject* exogeneity.
