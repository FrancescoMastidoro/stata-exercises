## Background
The NLSY 1980 cross-section contains 935 observations on young workers, including wages, education, IQ, family background, and labor market characteristics. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics when a regressor — like education — is likely correlated with unobserved ability, making OLS estimates biased.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize` on `lwage`, `educ`, `exper`, `iq`, `meduc`, and `feduc`. Note that `meduc` has 78 missing values and `feduc` has 194 missing values. How many observations remain after Stata drops missing values listwise when all six variables are included?

2. **Run OLS as a benchmark** — Regress `lwage` on `educ`, `exper`, `iq`, `black`, and `south` using `regress`. Record the coefficient on `educ` and its standard error. This is your baseline — it may be upward-biased if education is endogenous.

3. **First-stage regression** — Regress `educ` on the proposed instruments `meduc` and `feduc`, plus the controls `exper`, `iq`, `black`, and `south`. Report the F-statistic for the joint significance of `meduc` and `feduc` using `testparm`. A rule of thumb says instruments are strong when this F exceeds 10 — does yours?

4. **Two-stage least squares (2SLS)** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to instrument `educ` with `meduc` and `feduc`, controlling for `exper`, `iq`, `black`, and `south`. Compare the IV coefficient on `educ` to the OLS estimate from Task 2. Then run the Hausman endogeneity test using `estat endogenous` — is there statistical evidence that `educ` is endogenous?

5. *(Bonus)* **Overidentification test** — Because you have two instruments for one endogenous variable, the model is overidentified. Run `estat overid` after your `ivregress` command to obtain the Sargan–Hansen statistic. Interpret what a rejection of the null would mean for instrument validity. Then re-estimate using `ivregress liml` instead of `2sls` and compare coefficients — under what conditions would LIML be preferred?
