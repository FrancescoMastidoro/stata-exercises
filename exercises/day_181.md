## Background
The Mroz (1987) dataset contains information on married women's labor supply decisions, wages, and family characteristics drawn from the PSID. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is essential in labor economics when a regressor — like education — is endogenous due to ability bias or measurement error, and a valid instrument can isolate exogenous variation.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the key variables** — Load the dataset and summarize `wwage76`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `husage`. Report the mean and standard deviation of each. Then tabulate `lfp` to understand how many wives participate in the labor force.

2. **OLS benchmark** — Among wives who worked (`lfp == 1`), regress `wwage76` on `wedyrs`, `wexper`, and `kl6` using OLS. Note the coefficient on `wedyrs` and its standard error — this is your naive estimate potentially contaminated by ability bias.

3. **First-stage regression** — Run the first stage of 2SLS manually: regress `wedyrs` on your proposed instrument `wmedyrs` (wife's mother's years of education), plus the controls `wexper` and `kl6`, restricting to `lfp == 1`. Report the F-statistic on the excluded instrument and comment on instrument relevance (a common rule of thumb is F > 10).

4. **IV estimation via `ivregress`** — Now estimate the structural wage equation using `ivregress 2sls wwage76 (wedyrs = wmedyrs) wexper kl6 if lfp == 1`. Compare the IV coefficient on `wedyrs` to your OLS estimate from Task 2. Then run `estat firststage` to retrieve the first-stage F-statistic automatically, and run `estat endogtest` to test whether `wedyrs` is statistically endogenous.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add a second instrument and test overidentification** — Re-estimate the IV model adding `wfedyrs` (wife's father's years of education) as a second instrument: `ivregress 2sls wwage76 (wedyrs = wmedyrs wfedyrs) wexper kl6 if lfp == 1`. Run `estat overid` to perform the Sargan–Hansen overidentification test. Interpret the p-value: does the test cast doubt on the validity of at least one instrument?

If you run any other estimation command in between, you will need to re-run `ivregress` first. The endogeneity test null hypothesis is that the suspected endogenous regressor (`wedyrs`) can actually be treated as exogenous — a small p-value is evidence of endogeneity.
