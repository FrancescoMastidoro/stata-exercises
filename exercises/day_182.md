## Background
The Grunfeld dataset is a classic balanced panel of 10 large U.S. firms observed annually from 1935 to 1954, containing information on investment, market value, and capital stock. Difference-in-differences (DiD) estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor and industrial economics for recovering causal effects when random assignment is not feasible, exploiting variation across groups and time.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare the panel** — Load the Grunfeld dataset, then declare it as a panel using the appropriate identifiers (`company` and `year`). Confirm the panel is strongly balanced using `xtdescribe`.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company` ≤ 5 (the "treated group") and 0 otherwise. Then create a binary variable `post` equal to 1 for years from 1945 onward and 0 for earlier years. These two variables will form the basis of your DiD design.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run a basic DiD regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Interpret the coefficient on `did` — what does it represent in a DiD framework?

4. **Re-estimate with two-way fixed effects** — Instead of manually including `treated` and `post`, use `xtreg` with fixed effects (`fe`) to absorb company fixed effects, and include `post` and `did` as regressors. Then add `i.year` as additional controls to absorb year fixed effects as well. Compare the coefficient on `did` to your result in Task 3 and reflect on why they might differ.

5. *(Bonus)* **Visualize parallel trends** — Collapse the data to compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (`treated`) and `year`. Plot the mean investment over time separately for the treated and control groups using `twoway line`. Does the pre-1945 trend look similar for both groups, providing suggestive support for the parallel trends assumption?
