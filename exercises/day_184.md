## Background
The PSID extract tracks 595 individuals over 7 time periods, recording log wages, union status, occupation, and demographic characteristics — a classic setup for panel-based causal inference. Difference-in-differences (DiD) exploits variation in treatment timing or group membership across time to identify causal effects, making it one of the most widely used tools in labor economics.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel structure** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel with `id` as the individual identifier and `t` as the time variable. Confirm the panel is balanced by running `xtdescribe`.

2. **Define treatment and post indicators** — Union membership (`union`) varies within individuals over time. Create a binary variable `ever_union` equal to 1 for individuals who are union members in *any* period (use `egen` with `max` over `id`). Then create a `post` indicator equal to 1 when `t >= 4` (the second half of the panel). Tabulate `ever_union` to see how many individuals are in the treatment group.

3. **Estimate a naive DiD** — Run a basic DiD regression of `lwage` on `ever_union`, `post`, and their interaction (`ever_union#post`). Use `regress` with robust standard errors. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term as the DiD estimate.

4. **Add individual and time fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD using `xtreg lwage post ever_union#post i.t, fe vce(robust)`. Explain in a comment why including individual fixed effects (`fe`) changes the estimate relative to Task 3, and what the `ever_union` main effect drops out of the model.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using only the pre-treatment periods (`t < 4`), compute the mean of `lwage` by `t` and `ever_union` separately using `collapse` (save to a tempfile and restore after). Then sketch what you would expect to see in a parallel trends plot, and run a regression of `lwage` on `ever_union#i.t` for `t < 4` to test whether the pre-trends coefficients are jointly zero using `testparm`.
