## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, including women's earnings, education, their partner's education, number of children, and other income. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of modern labor economics, allowing researchers to recover causal estimates of endogenous regressors — such as female education — by exploiting variation from a valid external instrument.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Note the ranges of `fem_inc`, `fem_educ`, `male_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc`. Then estimate a simple OLS regression of `fem_inc` on `fem_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc`.

2. **Check instrument relevance** — Run a simple OLS regression of `fem_educ` on `male_educ`, controlling for `kids` and `other_inc`. Report and interpret the coefficient on `male_educ`: is partner's education a strong predictor of female education? Check whether the t-statistic is large enough to suggest relevance.

3. **Estimate the IV model** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to regress `fem_inc` on `kids` and `other_inc`, instrumenting `fem_educ` with `male_educ`. Store the estimates with `estimates store iv_model`. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_educ` with the OLS coefficient from Task 1 — in which direction does the estimate move, and what might that imply?

4. **Test instrument validity diagnostics** — After the `ivregress` command, run `estat firststage` to examine the first-stage F-statistic. Then run `estat endogtest` to test whether `fem_educ` is statistically endogenous. Interpret both outputs: does the evidence support using IV here?

5. *(Bonus)* **Compare 2SLS and LIML** — Re-estimate the IV model using `ivregress liml` with the same specification. Use `estimates store liml_model` and then `estimates table iv_model liml_model, b se` to compare coefficients and standard errors across the two estimators. In settings with a single instrument and a single endogenous variable, what relationship do you expect between 2SLS and LIML estimates?

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: The `ivregress 2sls` syntax places the endogenous regressor and its instrument inside parentheses: `ivregress 2sls depvar exogvars (endogvar = instrument)`. Make sure `fem_educ` appears inside the parentheses paired with `male_educ`, while `kids` and `other_inc` remain outside as exogenous controls.

- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: `estat firststage` and `estat endogtest` are post-estimation commands that only work immediately after `ivregress`. A first-stage F-statistic below 10 is a common rule-of-thumb threshold for "weak instrument" concern — check where yours falls. The endogeneity test reports a p-value: a small p-value means you reject the null that the regressor is exogenous.
