## Background
The Grunfeld investment dataset tracks 10 U.S. firms across 20 years (1935–1954), recording real investment, market value, and capital stock — a classic panel used to study firm-level investment behavior. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) designs exploit variation in treatment timing across units and time, making panel data like this ideal for practicing the method's mechanics and assumptions.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and declare the panel** — Load the dataset with `webuse grunfeld`, then use `xtset` to declare it as a panel with `company` as the unit identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm the panel is strongly balanced by running `xtdescribe`.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Suppose a hypothetical policy was introduced in 1945 and affected only companies 1–5 (the "treated group"). Create a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1–5 and 0 otherwise. Then create a binary variable `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 for earlier years. Finally, generate the interaction term `did = treated * post`.

3. **Run the baseline DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using `regress`. Report the coefficient on `did` and write a comment in your do-file explaining what it measures economically.

4. **Add company and year fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD using `xtreg` with the `fe` option (which absorbs company fixed effects) and manually include year dummies using `i.year`. Compare the coefficient on `did` with the result from Task 3 and comment on why the estimates differ.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends visualization** — Compute the average investment by year separately for treated and control companies using `collapse` (saving the collapsed data) and then plot both series over time using `twoway line`. Visually inspect whether the parallel trends assumption looks plausible in the pre-1945 period.
