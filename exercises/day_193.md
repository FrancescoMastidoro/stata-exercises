## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, covering variables such as female income, education, husband's education, number of kids, and other household income. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of causal inference in labor economics, and combining it with LASSO-based instrument selection reflects modern best practices for avoiding weak-instrument problems in empirical work.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset with `webuse laborsup`. Use `describe` and `summarize` to understand the structure. Note the range of `fem_inc` (10–84.6), `fem_educ` (8–16), and `kids` (0–4). Tabulate `fem_work` to see the share of women in work.

2. **Run a baseline OLS regression** — Regress `fem_inc` on `fem_educ`, `kids`, and `other_inc`. Interpret the sign and magnitude of each coefficient. Store the estimates with `estimates store ols`.

3. **Estimate an IV model** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to regress `fem_inc` on `fem_educ` and `kids`, instrumenting for `fem_educ` with `male_educ`. Include `other_inc` as an exogenous control. After estimation, run `estat firststage` to check instrument strength and comment on whether the instrument appears weak.

4. **Test instrument validity and compare specifications** — Run `estat endogtest` to test whether `fem_educ` is endogenous. Then use `estimates store iv` and `estimates table ols iv, b se` to compare the OLS and IV coefficient estimates side by side. In a comment, explain what the difference in the `fem_educ` coefficient between OLS and IV suggests about potential bias.

5. *(Bonus)* **LASSO-assisted instrument selection** — Use Stata's `dsregress` (or `ivlasso` if available in your installation) to let LASSO select among `male_educ`, `other_inc`, and `kids` as potential instruments or controls for predicting `fem_educ`. Compare which variables LASSO retains to the instrument you chose manually in Task 3, and briefly discuss whether data-driven selection changes your inference about `fem_inc`.

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: The syntax for `ivregress 2sls` places endogenous regressors and their instruments inside parentheses: `ivregress 2sls y x_exog (x_endog = instrument)`. Run `estat firststage` immediately after — look at the F-statistic and compare it to the conventional threshold of 10.

- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: `estat endogtest` performs a Wu-Hausman test; a small p-value means you reject exogeneity of `fem_educ` and IV is preferred. When comparing OLS and IV estimates, remember that OLS is biased upward if education is positively correlated with unobserved ability — ask yourself whether the IV coefficient is larger or smaller than OLS and what that implies.
