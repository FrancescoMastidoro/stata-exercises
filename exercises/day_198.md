## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains firm-level investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 US companies observed annually from 1935 to 1954 — a classic panel used in econometrics textbooks. Difference-in-differences (DiD) estimation is central to modern program evaluation in labor and industrial economics, and practicing it on a clean balanced panel helps build the intuition before applying it to messier real-world data.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1 through 5 (the "treated" group) and 0 for companies 6 through 10. Then define a post-period variable `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 otherwise. Verify the counts in each cell of a two-way tabulation of `treated` × `post`.

3. **Construct the DiD interaction term and run the baseline regression** — Generate the interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using `reg`. Report and interpret the sign and magnitude of the DiD coefficient on `did`.

4. **Add firm and year fixed effects** — Re-estimate the model using `xtreg, fe` with `absorb`-style year dummies (include `i.year` as regressors) to control for unobserved firm heterogeneity and common time shocks. Compare the DiD coefficient to the estimate from Task 3 and briefly explain why it differs.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends inspection** — Create a variable `period` that equals `year - 1944` (so the post period begins at 1). For each value of `period` from -9 to 10, use `egen` to compute the group means of `invest` for treated and control companies separately, then plot both series with `twoway line` to visually assess whether pre-treatment trends look parallel. Comment on what you observe.
