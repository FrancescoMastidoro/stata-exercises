## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, including women's income, education, and family characteristics. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of labor economics, allowing researchers to recover causal estimates of endogenous regressors — such as female income — when OLS is likely biased due to omitted variables or simultaneity.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Note the ranges of `fem_educ`, `male_educ`, `fem_inc`, `other_inc`, and `kids`. Which variables could plausibly affect female income (`fem_inc`) but operate *through* a channel separate from labor supply choices (`fem_work`)?

2. **Estimate a naïve OLS model** — Regress `fem_work` (whether the woman works) on `fem_inc`, `kids`, and `other_inc`. Record the coefficient on `fem_inc` and its standard error. Briefly comment in a code note (`//`) on why OLS may be unreliable here — consider whether `fem_inc` is likely exogenous with respect to `fem_work`.

3. **Run a first-stage regression** — Regress `fem_inc` on your proposed instrument (`male_educ`), plus the controls `kids` and `other_inc`. Check whether the instrument is relevant by inspecting the F-statistic (use `estat firststage` after `ivregress`). A conventional rule of thumb: F > 10 suggests a strong instrument.

4. **Estimate the IV (2SLS) model** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to estimate the effect of `fem_inc` on `fem_work`, instrumenting for `fem_inc` with `male_educ`, and including `kids` and `other_inc` as controls. Compare the IV coefficient on `fem_inc` to the OLS estimate from Task 2 — does the direction or magnitude change, and what might that imply economically?

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the exclusion restriction indirectly and explore sensitivity** — Add `fem_educ` as an additional instrument alongside `male_educ` and re-estimate the IV model. Then use `estat overid` to run a Sargan–Hansen overidentification test. Interpret the p-value: what does a failure to reject tell you about the joint validity of the instruments, and what caveat should you keep in mind even if the test passes?

## Hints

- **Hint 1 (Task 3)**: After running `ivregress 2sls fem_work (fem_inc = male_educ) kids other_inc`, type `estat firststage` on the next line — Stata will report the first-stage F-statistic for the excluded instrument automatically.
- **Hint 2 (Task 4)**: The syntax for `ivregress 2sls` places the endogenous variable in parentheses together with its instrument: `(endogenous_var = instrument)`. Everything outside the parentheses is treated as exogenous controls included in both stages.
- **Hint 3 (Bonus)**: When you have more instruments than endogenous variables the model is *overidentified*, which is precisely the condition required for `estat overid` to be valid. Make sure you list both instruments inside the parentheses separated by a space before running the test.
