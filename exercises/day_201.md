## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations on workers observed across up to 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographics. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) designs exploit variation in treatment timing across units to identify causal effects, making panel data like this ideal for studying how labor market events — such as unionization — affect log wages over time.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure (unit: `id`, time: `t`). Confirm the declaration with `xtdescribe` and note how many panels are balanced vs. unbalanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary "post" variable equal to 1 for observations where `t >= 5` (the later periods of the panel), and 0 otherwise. Then tabulate `union` by `post` to inspect whether union membership shifted across periods.

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Estimate a DiD model using `xtreg` with fixed effects (`fe`) where the outcome is `lwage`, the treatment is `union`, the time indicator is `post`, and you include the interaction `union#c.post` (or equivalently `c.union#c.post`). Interpret the sign and magnitude of the interaction coefficient.

4. **Add controls and cluster standard errors** — Re-run the fixed-effects DiD model from Task 3, adding `wks`, `occ`, `ind`, `south`, and `smsa` as controls. Cluster standard errors at the `id` level using `vce(cluster id)`. Compare the interaction coefficient and its standard error to those from Task 3.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption informally** — Restrict the sample to the pre-treatment periods (`t < 5`) only. Within this subsample, regress `lwage` on `union`, `t`, and their interaction `c.union#c.t` using `xtreg, fe`. A parallel trends assumption requires that the interaction term is statistically indistinguishable from zero. Report and interpret your result.
