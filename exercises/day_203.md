## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed between 1968 and 1988, containing log wages, education, experience, and demographic controls across many survey years. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is one of the most widely used quasi-experimental strategies in labor economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing across groups and time to identify causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel with `xtset` using `idcode` and `year`, then summarize the key outcome variable `ln_wage` and the time variable `year`. How many distinct years appear in the data?

2. **Define a treatment indicator** — Create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `year >= 78` and 0 otherwise. This will serve as the "post-treatment" period indicator in your DiD design. Tabulate `year` against `post` to confirm it is correctly defined.

3. **Define a treated group** — Create a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 if an individual is ever observed living in a non-SMSA area (`not_smsa == 1`) in the pre-period (`year < 78`), and 0 otherwise. To do this, use `egen` to generate a group-level maximum of `not_smsa` in the pre-period within each `idcode`. Tabulate `treated` to check how many individuals fall into each group.

4. **Estimate a basic DiD regression** — Run an OLS regression of `ln_wage` on `treated`, `post`, their interaction `treated#post` (or construct `treated_post = treated * post` manually), and `ttl_exp` as a control. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term as your DiD estimate. Use `robust` standard errors.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add individual fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg, fe` with `robust` standard errors, absorbing individual fixed effects. Compare the interaction coefficient to Task 4. Why does the `treated` main effect drop out when fixed effects are included? How does controlling for unobserved individual heterogeneity change your estimate?
