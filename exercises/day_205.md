## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, including women's and husbands' education, number of children, non-labor income, and female earnings. Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a cornerstone of empirical labor economics because it allows researchers to recover causal effects when OLS suffers from endogeneity — a situation that arises frequently when studying wages, education, and labor supply decisions.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Identify which variable could serve as the outcome (female income), which as the potentially endogenous regressor (number of kids), and which demographic variables might serve as controls. Write a brief comment in your do-file explaining why `kids` might be endogenous in a regression of `fem_inc` on `kids`.

2. **Run a naive OLS regression** — Regress `fem_inc` on `kids`, `other_inc`, `fem_educ`, and `male_educ`. Store the OLS coefficient on `kids` and note its sign and magnitude. This is your baseline to which you will compare IV estimates.

3. **First-stage regression** — Propose `male_educ` as an instrument for `kids` (the argument: husband's education affects household fertility decisions but may not directly determine the wife's income, conditional on controls). Run the first-stage regression of `kids` on `male_educ`, `other_inc`, and `fem_educ`. Examine the F-statistic and the sign of the coefficient on `male_educ` to assess instrument relevance.

4. **IV estimation with `ivregress`** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to regress `fem_inc` on `other_inc` and `fem_educ`, instrumenting `kids` with `male_educ`. Compare the IV coefficient on `kids` to the OLS coefficient from Task 2. Then run the Durbin–Wu–Hausman endogeneity test using the appropriate post-estimation command and interpret whether endogeneity is a statistically significant concern at the 5% level.

5. *(Bonus)* **Weak instrument diagnostics** — After re-running `ivregress 2sls` with the `first` option, check whether `male_educ` passes the conventional rule-of-thumb threshold for a strong instrument (F > 10). Then add `fem_educ` as a second instrument alongside `male_educ`, re-estimate the IV model, and run an overidentification test using `estat overid`. Interpret the p-value: does the overidentification test raise concerns about instrument validity?
