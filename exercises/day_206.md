## Background
The Mroz 1987 PSID dataset contains detailed information on married women's labor supply, wages, education, and family characteristics — a classic benchmark in labor economics. Instrumental Variables (IV) estimation is essential here because a wife's wage is endogenous to her labor supply decision, requiring instruments that shift wages without directly affecting hours worked.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and summarize `wwage76`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `whrs75`, and `kl6`. Note the ranges and describe in a comment what each variable represents in the labor supply context.

2. **OLS benchmark** — Regress `whrs75` (wife's hours worked in 1975) on `wwage75`, `wedyrs`, `wexper`, `kl6`, and `k618` using OLS. Store these results with `estimates store ols`. Interpret the coefficient on `wwage75` in a comment.

3. **IV estimation** — Re-estimate the model from Task 2 using `ivregress 2sls`, instrumenting `wwage75` with `weduc` and `wmeduc` (wife's college dummy and mother's college dummy). Store results as `estimates store iv`. Compare the IV coefficient on `wwage75` to the OLS coefficient — is the direction of bias consistent with what you would expect from omitted ability?

4. **Test instrument validity** — After your `ivregress` command, run `estat firststage` to inspect the first-stage F-statistic. Then run `estat overid` to test the overidentification restriction. In a comment, state whether the instruments appear strong and whether the overidentification test raises concerns.

5. *(Bonus)* **Hausman test** — Use `hausman iv ols` (in that order) to formally test whether the OLS and IV estimates differ significantly. Interpret the p-value: if we reject H₀, what does this imply about the exogeneity of `wwage75`? Note any practical difficulties in computing this test and how `ivregress`'s built-in `endogtest` option could serve as an alternative.
