## Background
The Grunfeld dataset tracks investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large U.S. firms over 20 years (1935–1954), making it a canonical panel for studying firm-level dynamics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) designs exploit variation in treatment timing or status across units to identify causal effects, and practicing DiD on a clean, well-understood panel helps build the intuition needed for applied labor economics work.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel with `xtset`, and use `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is strongly balanced. Report the number of units and time periods.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company <= 5` (the "treated" group) and 0 otherwise. Then create a post-period indicator `post` equal to 1 for years after 1944 (i.e., `year > 1944`) and 0 otherwise. Finally, generate the interaction term `did = treated * post`.

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`, using robust standard errors. Interpret the coefficient on `did` as the average treatment effect on investment for the treated group in the post-period.

4. **Add controls and firm fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg` with firm fixed effects (`fe`), adding `mvalue` and `kstock` as time-varying controls and clustering standard errors at the company level. Compare the DiD coefficient to the estimate from Task 3 and explain conceptually why the two estimates differ.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends check** — To assess the parallel trends assumption, generate year-relative-to-treatment dummies (one for each year from 1935 to 1954, omitting 1944 as the reference year) interacted with `treated`, and run an event-study regression of `invest` on these interaction terms plus firm fixed effects. Use `coefplot` (if installed) or manually inspect the pre-period coefficients to discuss whether pre-trends appear problematic.

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: The panel identifiers in this dataset are `company` and `year` — pass them in that order to `xtset`. After declaring the panel, `xtdescribe` will tell you whether every unit is observed in every period (look for the "strongly balanced" message).

- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: In a canonical 2×2 DiD, `treated` captures the pre-period level difference between groups, `post` captures the common time trend, and the coefficient on the interaction `did` is your causal estimate of interest. Make sure you understand *which* comparison each coefficient encodes before moving to Task 4.

- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: When you use `xtreg, fe`, Stata absorbs all time-invariant unit characteristics — this means the main effect of `treated` (which is constant within a firm) drops out automatically. Use `vce(cluster company)` to account for serial correlation within firms. Think about why the fixed-effects estimate might be closer to or further from the OLS estimate depending on whether unobserved firm heterogeneity is correlated with group membership.

- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: One common way to construct event-study interactions is to loop over years, generate dummies `i.year == y` for each `y`, interact them with `treated`, and include all of them (except the reference year) as regressors. The pre-period coefficients should be statistically indistinguishable from zero if parallel trends holds.
