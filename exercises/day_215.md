## Background
The PSID extract contains 595 individuals observed across 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographic characteristics — a classic structure for panel-based causal inference. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) exploits variation in treatment timing or group membership across time to identify causal effects, making it one of the most widely used methods in labor economics.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `id` as the individual identifier and `t` as the time variable. Confirm the setup with `xtdescribe` and report how many individuals and periods are in the data.

2. **Define treatment and post indicators** — Union membership (`union`) varies across individuals and time. Create a binary variable `ever_union` equal to 1 if an individual is ever observed as a union member across all their periods (use `egen` with the `max` function over the panel), and a binary variable `post` equal to 1 for periods `t >= 4`. Display the number of individuals who are "ever union" vs. "never union."

3. **Run a basic DiD regression** — Regress `lwage` on `ever_union`, `post`, and their interaction `ever_union##post` using `xtreg` with the `fe` option to absorb individual fixed effects. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term: what does it estimate?

4. **Add time fixed effects and controls** — Extend the model by adding `i.t` (time dummies) and the control variables `ed`, `exp`, `south`, and `smsa`. Use `xtreg, fe` again. Compare the interaction coefficient with the one from Task 3 and comment on how controlling for time trends and observables changes the estimate.

5. *(Bonus)* **Parallel trends check** — To partially assess the parallel trends assumption, restrict the sample to pre-treatment periods (`t < 4`) and run an event-study style regression by interacting `ever_union` with each time dummy (`i.t#c.ever_union`). Do the interaction coefficients for pre-treatment periods appear statistically different from zero? What would concern you if they were?

- **Hint 1 (Task 2)**: The `egen` function you need is `egen ever_union = max(union), by(id)` — think about why taking the maximum of a 0/1 variable within a person gives you "ever treated."
- **Hint 2 (Task 3)**: When you use `xtreg, fe`, Stata absorbs the individual fixed effect, so `ever_union` on its own will be dropped (it is time-invariant within person once you use `ever_union` as defined). Focus your interpretation on the interaction term `1.ever_union#1.post`, which is the DiD estimator.
- **Hint 3 (Task 4)**: You can include time fixed effects in an `xtreg, fe` model by simply adding `i.t` to the regressor list — Stata handles the within-group demeaning automatically alongside time dummies.
- **Hint 4 (Task 5)**: Restrict observations with `if t < 4` and replace `post` with individual time dummies interacted with `ever_union`. Near-zero and insignificant pre-trend coefficients support (but do not prove) the parallel trends assumption.
