## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed across survey years from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, work experience, and demographic characteristics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing across groups and time to estimate causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset`, and summarize the key outcome variable `ln_wage` and the binary variable `black`. Report how many unique individuals and how many time periods are represented.

2. **Create a post-treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `post` equal to 1 if `year` is greater than or equal to 78 (i.e., 1978 onward), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `post` against `year` to verify that the cutoff is coded correctly.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run a basic DiD** — Generate an interaction variable `did = black * post`. Run an OLS regression of `ln_wage` on `black`, `post`, and `did`. Interpret the coefficient on `did` as the DiD estimate — does it suggest a narrowing or widening of the Black–White wage gap after 1978?

4. **Add controls and cluster standard errors** — Re-run the DiD regression adding `ttl_exp`, `grade`, and `south` as controls. Cluster standard errors at the `idcode` level. Compare the DiD coefficient and its standard error to those from Task 3 — how does controlling for experience and education affect the estimate?

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Collapse the data to cell means of `ln_wage` by `black` and `year`. Plot the two wage trends over time (one line for `black == 1`, one for `black == 0`) using `twoway`. Do the pre-1978 trends look approximately parallel? Comment on what a violation of this assumption would mean for your DiD estimate.
