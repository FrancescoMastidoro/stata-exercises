## Background
The PSID extract contains 595 individuals observed across 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographic characteristics — a classic structure for panel-based causal inference. Difference-in-differences (DiD) exploits variation in treatment timing or group assignment across panel observations to isolate causal effects, making it a cornerstone method in labor economics.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load `webuse psidextract`, then declare the panel structure using `xtset`. Confirm the panel dimensions: how many individuals, how many periods, and what is the balanced panel size?

2. **Define treatment and control groups** — Union membership (`union`) is your treatment indicator. Using `tabstat lwage`, compute the mean log wage separately for union and non-union workers, and separately for the first period (`t == 1`) and the last period (`t == 7`). Arrange your results so you can read off the four cell means needed for a simple 2×2 DiD calculation.

3. **Compute the DiD estimate by hand** — Using the four cell means you found in Task 2, manually compute the DiD estimate: (mean log wage for union workers in t=7 − t=1) − (mean log wage for non-union workers in t=7 − t=1). Store this number somewhere (a comment is fine) — you will compare it to a regression estimate in Task 4.

4. **Estimate DiD with a regression** — Run a regression of `lwage` on `union`, a dummy for the last period (`t == 7`), and their interaction. Use `xtreg` with fixed effects (`fe`) rather than pooled OLS to absorb individual-level unobservables. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term and compare it to your hand-calculated estimate from Task 3. Why do the two numbers differ?

5. *(Bonus)* **Add covariates and test parallel trends** — Re-estimate the fixed-effects DiD model from Task 4, adding `ed`, `blk`, `south`, and `occ` as controls. Then test an informal version of the parallel trends assumption: restrict the sample to periods `t == 1` through `t == 6` only, create a "placebo" last-period dummy (`t == 6`), and check whether its interaction with `union` is statistically significant. What would a large, significant coefficient imply about your DiD design?

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse psidextract`, use `xtset id t` to declare the panel. Then `xtdescribe` or `xtsum` will show you balanced panel details — check whether every individual appears in every period.

- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: `tabstat lwage, by(union) stat(mean)` gives union vs non-union means overall. To get period-specific means, consider adding `if t == 1` and `if t == 7` to separate calls, or explore `table t union, stat(mean lwage)` for a compact 2×2 layout.

- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: The 2×2 DiD formula is (Treated\_Post − Treated\_Pre) − (Control\_Post − Control\_Pre). Write the arithmetic explicitly as a comment in your do-file so the logic is transparent.

- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: For `xtreg ... fe`, the interaction term is `union * (t==7)`. You will need to generate a binary variable for the last period first (e.g., `gen post = (t == 7)`), then include `union`, `post`, and `c.union#c.post` — or simply `union##post` using factor-variable notation. Remember that `xtreg, fe` absorbs time-constant individual characteristics, which the simple 2×2 means do not.
