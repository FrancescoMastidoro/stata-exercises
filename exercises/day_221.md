## Background
The Grunfeld dataset contains firm-level investment, market value, and capital stock for 10 large US corporations observed annually from 1935 to 1954 — a classic balanced panel used extensively in applied econometrics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics and policy evaluation, and practicing it on a clean panel helps build the intuition before applying it to messier real-world data.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset, then use `xtset` to declare `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Display the panel structure summary and confirm the panel is strongly balanced.

2. **Create a treatment indicator** — Define a binary variable `treated` equal to 1 for companies with `company` ≤ 5 (the "treated group") and 0 otherwise. Then create a `post` indicator equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 for earlier years. Tabulate the two-way cross of `treated` and `post` to verify cell counts.

3. **Compute the raw DiD estimate by hand** — Using `summarize` with `if` conditions, calculate the mean of `invest` for each of the four cells (treated×post, treated×pre, control×post, control×pre). Compute the DiD estimate manually as (mean_treated_post − mean_treated_pre) − (mean_control_post − mean_control_pre) and store the result in a local macro called `did_manual`. Display it with `display`.

4. **Run the two-way fixed effects DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on the interaction `treated#post`, controlling for company fixed effects (`i.company`) and year fixed effects (`i.year`). Use `xtreg` with the `fe` option *or* `regress` with `absorb`-style dummies, and cluster standard errors at the `company` level. Interpret the coefficient on `1.treated#1.post` relative to your manual estimate from Task 3 — why do the two numbers differ?

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Restrict the sample to the pre-treatment period (years before 1945). Using `collapse`, compute mean `invest` by `treated` and `year` for the pre-period only. Then use `twoway line` to plot the two group trends on the same graph. Comment in your do-file on whether the pre-trends look parallel, and what that implies for the validity of your DiD design.
