## Background
The `laborsup` dataset contains 500 observations on female labor supply, including women's income, education, husband's education, number of children, and other household income. Instrumental variables estimation is a cornerstone technique in labor economics for recovering causal effects when regressors are endogenous, and combining IV with LASSO-based instrument selection is an increasingly common approach in modern applied work.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the data** — Load the dataset and run `describe` and `summarize`. Identify which variables could plausibly serve as instruments for `fem_inc` when modeling `fem_work` as the outcome. Write a comment in your do-file explaining your reasoning for at least one candidate instrument.

2. **Estimate a baseline probit and OLS model** — Regress `fem_work` on `fem_inc`, `kids`, and `other_inc` using both `probit` and `regress`. Compare the sign and approximate magnitude of the coefficient on `fem_inc` across the two models. Note any differences you observe in a comment.

3. **Run a 2SLS (IV) regression** — Use `ivregress 2sls` to instrument for `fem_inc` using `male_educ` and `fem_educ` as instruments, controlling for `kids` and `other_inc`. After estimation, run `estat endogtest` to test whether `fem_inc` is actually endogenous, and `estat firststage` to assess instrument strength. Report what the first-stage F-statistic suggests about instrument relevance.

4. **Test overidentifying restrictions** — After your IV regression from Task 3, run `estat overid` to perform the Sargan–Hansen test. Interpret the p-value: does it support the validity of your instrument set? Add a comment explaining what a rejection of this test would imply for your identification strategy.

5. *(Bonus)* **LASSO-based instrument selection** — Use `lasso linear fem_inc (kids other_inc = fem_educ male_educ)` via the `dsregress` command (or `ivlasso` if available in your Stata version) to let LASSO select among candidate instruments and controls. Compare the resulting coefficient on `fem_inc` to your 2SLS estimate. Discuss in a comment whether the two approaches agree on the direction and magnitude of the effect.
