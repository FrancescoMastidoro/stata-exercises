## Background
The Grunfeld dataset is a classic balanced panel of 10 large US firms observed annually from 1935 to 1954, containing firm investment, market value, and capital stock. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is one of the most widely used quasi-experimental strategies in labor and applied economics, allowing you to estimate causal effects by comparing treated and control units before and after a policy change.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load the dataset, declare it as a panel using `xtset`, and summarize the key variables `invest`, `mvalue`, and `kstock`. Confirm that the panel is balanced (every firm appears in every year).

2. **Construct a treatment indicator** — Define a "treated" group as companies with `company` values 1–5, and a "post" period as years from 1945 onward. Create two new binary variables: `treated` (= 1 if company ≤ 5) and `post` (= 1 if year ≥ 1945). Then create their interaction term `did` = `treated` × `post`, which is the DiD variable of interest.

3. **Run the baseline DiD regression** — Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did`. Interpret the coefficient on `did`: what does it represent in a DiD framework? Note the number of observations and R-squared.

4. **Add controls and fixed effects** — Re-run the DiD regression adding `mvalue` and `kstock` as controls, and absorbing company fixed effects with `areg` (absorb on `company`). Compare the new `did` coefficient to the one from Task 3 — does controlling for firm-level heterogeneity change the estimate meaningfully?

5. *(Bonus)* **Inspect parallel trends visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (treated vs. control) and `year`. Then use `twoway line` to plot the average investment path for treated and control firms over time, drawing a vertical reference line at 1945 with `xline(1945)`. Do the pre-treatment trends look parallel?

## Hints

- **Hint 1 (Task 1)**: After `webuse grunfeld`, use `xtset company year` to declare the panel structure. The command `xtdescribe` will tell you whether the panel is balanced without having to count manually.

- **Hint 2 (Task 2)**: You can generate binary variables with conditions: `gen treated = (company <= 5)` creates a 0/1 variable directly — no `if` clause needed. The interaction term is simply the arithmetic product of the two binary variables.

- **Hint 3 (Task 3)**: In a standard DiD setup, the coefficient on the interaction term (`did`) captures the average treatment effect on the treated in the post period, net of time trends and group-level differences. Think carefully about what the `treated` and `post` coefficients mean on their own.

- **Hint 4 (Task 4)**: `areg invest treated post did mvalue kstock, absorb(company)` is the right structure. Notice that once you absorb company fixed effects, the standalone `treated` dummy will be dropped — why does that happen?
