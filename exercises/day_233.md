## Background
The PSID extract contains 4,165 observations tracking 595 individuals across up to 7 time periods, with information on wages, union status, occupation, and demographic characteristics. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for estimating causal effects by comparing within-person changes over time across groups that differ in their exposure to some treatment.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and explore the panel structure** — Load the dataset and use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `id` as the individual identifier and `t` as the time variable. Then use `xtdescribe` to confirm the panel is balanced and report how many individuals and periods are present.

2. **Define treatment and post indicators** — Union membership (`union`) will serve as the "treatment" in a simple DiD framework. Create a variable called `post` equal to 1 if `t >= 4` and 0 otherwise. Then construct the interaction term `did = union * post` manually using `generate`. Tabulate `union` and `post` together to see the cell counts.

3. **Run the baseline DiD regression** — Regress `lwage` on `union`, `post`, and `did`. Interpret the coefficient on `did` as the DiD estimate: does union membership in the post period appear to affect log wages? Report the coefficient and its statistical significance.

4. **Add individual fixed effects and controls** — Re-estimate the model using `xtreg lwage union post did exp wks ed, fe`. Compare the coefficient on `did` to the pooled OLS result from Task 3. Also add `robust` standard errors. Does controlling for time-invariant individual heterogeneity change the conclusion?

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption informally** — Restrict the sample to the pre-period (`t < 4`) only. Within this subsample, regress `lwage` on `union`, a linear time trend (`t`), and their interaction `union * t` using `xtreg, fe`. If the parallel trends assumption holds, what sign and magnitude would you expect for the interaction coefficient, and what do you actually find?
