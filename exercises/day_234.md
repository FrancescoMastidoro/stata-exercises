## Background
The Grunfeld dataset is a classic panel of 10 large U.S. firms observed annually from 1935 to 1954, containing firm investment, market value, and capital stock. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor and industrial economics, exploiting variation in treatment timing across units to estimate causal effects.

## Your tasks

1. **Declare the panel structure** — Load the dataset and declare it as a panel using `xtset`, with `company` as the panel identifier and `year` as the time variable. Verify the declaration with `xtdescribe`.

2. **Construct a treatment indicator** — Create a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 for companies 1–5 (the "treated" group) and 0 for companies 6–10. Then create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 for years 1945 and later, and 0 otherwise. These will serve as your DiD building blocks.

3. **Construct the interaction term and run a basic DiD regression** — Generate an interaction variable `did = treated * post`. Regress `invest` on `treated`, `post`, and `did` using OLS (`regress`). Report and interpret the coefficient on `did` in a comment: what does it represent in a DiD framework?

4. **Improve the specification with fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg` with the `fe` option to absorb company fixed effects. Include `post` and `did` as regressors (drop the `treated` dummy — explain in a comment why it drops out). Compare the coefficient on `did` to your Task 3 estimate. Also cluster standard errors at the `company` level using `vce(cluster company)`.

5. *(Bonus)* **Test the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `collapse`, compute the mean of `invest` by `company` group (treated vs. control) and `year`. Then use `twoway` to plot the mean investment trends for treated and control groups on the same graph over time, adding a vertical reference line at 1945 with `xline(1945)`. Do the pre-treatment trends look parallel? Add a brief comment in your do-file.
