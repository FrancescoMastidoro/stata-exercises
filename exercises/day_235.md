## Background
The `regsmpl` dataset is a panel of NLS women observed across survey years from 1968 to 1988, containing wages, education, work experience, and regional identifiers. Difference-in-differences (DiD) is a cornerstone method in labor economics for identifying causal effects by comparing changes over time across groups, making this longitudinal dataset an ideal training ground.

## Your tasks

1. **Load and inspect the panel** — Load `webuse regsmpl`, then use `xtset` to declare the panel structure with `idcode` as the individual identifier and `year` as the time variable. Confirm the declaration with `xtdescribe` and report the time range and number of individuals.

2. **Define treatment and control groups** — Create a binary variable called `treat` equal to 1 if the individual is `black` and 0 otherwise. Then create a binary variable called `post` equal to 1 if `year >= 80` (i.e., 1980 onwards) and 0 otherwise. Tabulate both variables to verify their distributions.

3. **Compute the two-by-two DiD estimate by hand** — Using `tabstat ln_wage`, compute the four group-period means of `ln_wage`: treated pre, treated post, control pre, control post. Then manually calculate the DiD estimate as (treated post − treated pre) − (control post − control pre). Report the result.

4. **Estimate the DiD regression** — Run an OLS regression of `ln_wage` on `treat`, `post`, and their interaction `treat#post`. Add `ttl_exp` as a control. Interpret the coefficient on the interaction term and compare it to your hand-computed estimate from Task 3.

5. *(Bonus)* **Add individual fixed effects** — Re-estimate the DiD model using `xtreg ln_wage post c.treat#c.post ttl_exp, fe`. Explain why `treat` drops out of the fixed-effects model, and discuss how the coefficient on the interaction term changes compared to the pooled OLS result.
