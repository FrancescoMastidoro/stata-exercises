## Background
The NLSY 1980 cross-section tracks 935 workers and records wages, education, and demographic characteristics useful for studying labor market returns. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a cornerstone identification strategy in labor economics, exploiting variation across groups and over time to estimate causal treatment effects while controlling for time-invariant confounders.

## Your tasks

1. **Explore the treatment and outcome variables** — Load the dataset and summarize `lwage`, `college`, `south`, `black`, and `exper`. Report the share of workers who are college graduates and the share living in the South. This gives you a sense of group composition before defining treatment and control groups.

2. **Define a synthetic treatment indicator** — Create a binary variable called `treated` equal to 1 if a worker is a college graduate (`college == 1`) *and* lives in the South (`south == 1`), and 0 otherwise. Tabulate `treated` against `college` and `south` to verify your construction produces the correct cell counts.

3. **Compute group means and a manual DiD estimate** — Using `tabstat`, compute the mean of `lwage` separately for all four cells defined by `college` (0/1) and `south` (0/1). Then manually calculate the DiD estimate: (mean\_college\_south − mean\_college\_nosouth) − (mean\_nocollege\_south − mean\_nocollege\_nosouth). Write the numeric result as a comment in your do-file and interpret its sign.

4. **Estimate the DiD model by regression** — Run a linear regression of `lwage` on `college`, `south`, and their interaction term `college#south` (use factor-variable notation). Confirm that the coefficient on the interaction term matches your manual calculation from Task 3. Add `black`, `exper`, and `married` as controls and report how the interaction coefficient changes, noting whether the estimate is robust to these covariates.

5. *(Bonus)* **Assess the parallel trends assumption visually** — Using `pcollege` as a pre-college-completion indicator, create a new variable `educ_group` equal to 1 for workers with `educ >= 14` (some college or above) and 0 otherwise. Within this grouping, compare mean `lwage` across `south` values using `tabstat`. Then run a regression of `lwage` on `educ_group`, `south`, `educ_group#south`, and `exper`, and interpret whether the interaction differs meaningfully from your main DiD estimate — discuss in comments what this implies about the validity of the parallel trends assumption.
